# Common Priority Areas

Initial common areas identified as cross-country themes for QAI Platform Development.

These areas provide a working taxonomy for connecting national and sector priorities to potential products, capabilities, use cases and platform development activities.

This is a working taxonomy, not a ranking.

~~~

## 1. Purpose

The Common Priority Areas provide a shared reference structure for identifying areas where similar technology, engineering and digital transformation needs may exist across multiple countries.

They provide an intermediate layer between:

```text
National / Regional Priorities
        ↓
Common Priority Areas
        ↓
Sector
        ↓
Problem / Opportunity
        ↓
Product
        ↓
Use Case
        ↓
Platform Capability
```

The taxonomy is intended to support exploration and mapping rather than determine government or policy priorities.

~~~

## 12. Common Area → Sector Mapping

A common priority area can apply to multiple sectors.

For example:

```text
AI and Digital Transformation
        ├── Agriculture
        ├── Manufacturing
        ├── Energy
        ├── Government
        ├── Defence
        └── Infrastructure
```

Similarly, a sector may map to multiple common areas.

```text
Agriculture
   ├── AI and Digital Transformation
   ├── Energy and Resource Efficiency
   ├── Compute and Digital Infrastructure
   ├── Workforce and Productivity
   └── Food Systems
```

The mapping is therefore many-to-many.

~~~

## 13. Common Area → Product Development

The taxonomy is intended to support product opportunity identification.

```text
Common Priority Area
        ↓
Sector
        ↓
Problem / Opportunity
        ↓
Product Opportunity
        ↓
Required Capability
        ↓
PaaS Engineering
        ↓
IaaS Requirements
        ↓
SaaS Product
```

A priority area does not automatically imply that a product should be developed.

Further problem, market, technical, evidence and commercialization analysis is required.

## 15. Relationship to Country Priorities

Each country has its own priority structure.

The common taxonomy provides a normalized reference layer without assuming that every country gives equal importance to every area.

```text
Country A Priorities ──┐
Country B Priorities ──┼──> Common Priority Areas
Country C Priorities ──┘
                              ↓
                         Sector Mapping
                              ↓
                        Product Analysis
```

Country-specific documents remain the authoritative source for country-specific priority descriptions.

~~~

## 16. Relationship to Product Catalogue

The common areas support the product catalogue but do not replace it.

```text

        ↓
```

The product catalogue should retain the traceability back to the relevant priority area where applicable.

## 18. Working Taxonomy

The taxonomy is intentionally extensible.

A new common area may be introduced when:

- It appears across multiple country analyses
- It represents a meaningful technology or engineering theme
- Existing categories cannot represent it adequately
- It creates a useful product-development grouping

Changes should be documented rather than silently changing the taxonomy.

~~~

## 19. Evidence and Traceability

Where a common area is derived from country research, the underlying country-specific evidence should remain traceable.

The common taxonomy is therefore a normalization and planning structure, not a replacement for the source material.

```text
Source / Country Evidence
        ↓
Country Priority
        ↓
Common Area Mapping
        ↓
Sector Mapping
        ↓
Product / Gap Analysis
```

~~~
