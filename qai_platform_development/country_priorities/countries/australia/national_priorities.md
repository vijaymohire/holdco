## Release Update — 3 October 2026: National Priorities Alignment

### 1. Purpose

This release extends the initial national priority baseline by mapping relevant Australian Government policy themes to the potential contribution areas of the FAEP–QAI industrial ecosystem.

The update is intended to support:
- Country-level strategy and capability mapping.
- QAI product, service, modernization and research alignment.
- Identification of potential industry pilot and demonstration opportunities.
- Evidence-based proposal development and partner discussions.
- Traceability between national priorities, technical capabilities and measurable outcomes.

This document is a planning reference. It does not imply government endorsement, confirmed procurement demand, program eligibility or funding approval.

### 4. FAEP–QAI Alignment Principles

National priorities should be translated into engineering and commercialization work through the following sequence:

**National challenge → Evidence and stakeholder needs → Use case → Existing capability assessment → Pilot design → Validation → Commercialization decision.**

Apply these principles:

- **Problem-first:** identify an evidenced industry or public-sector need before selecting a technology.
- **People-first:** augment human capability, improve safety and support meaningful work.
- **Reuse before duplication:** assess existing infrastructure, software, standards and partner capabilities.
- **Interoperability:** prefer modular systems and integration with existing operational environments.
- **Responsible automation:** define human oversight, safety boundaries, security and accountability.
- **Evidence-based quantum adoption:** compare quantum or hybrid approaches with appropriate classical baselines; do not assume quantum advantage.
- **Staged investment:** progress from simulation and synthetic data to emulation, controlled pilots and physical deployment where justified.
- **Commercial discipline:** define measurable value, lifecycle costs, IP boundaries and a route to market.

### 5. National Priority to Capability Traceability

For each priority or proposed opportunity, maintain a record containing:

- Priority identifier and description.
- Official source URL, publication date and access/review date.
- Exact policy statement or relevant passage.
- Evidence classification: official fact, stakeholder requirement, hypothesis or internal proposal.
- Related FAEP–QAI capability or product family.
- Existing capability versus development gap.
- Potential industry or research partner.
- Proposed use case and pilot stage.
- Baseline metrics, success criteria and validation method.
- IP ownership, disclosure constraints and licensing considerations.
- Relevant program or procurement pathway, if independently verified.
- Next action, owner and review date.

Do not treat a broad national priority as proof that a specific product is needed or that a particular funding program will accept an application.
