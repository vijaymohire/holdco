## 1. Objectives

This directory supports a structured assessment of how QAI / FAEP capabilities may address validated needs in ACT Government, local industry, research, and community services.

It aims to:

- Understand ACT Government technology and economic development directions.
- Identify relevant digital, AI, cybersecurity, data, and infrastructure requirements.
- Understand supplier registration, procurement pathways, and future tender opportunities.
- Map candidate QAI / FAEP capabilities to defined problems and stakeholder needs.
- Assess existing systems, reuse opportunities, integration requirements, and implementation gaps.
- Identify potential pilots and partnerships with measurable outcomes.
- Record authoritative sources, applicability, evidence, risks, and follow-up actions.

**Important:** Policy alignment is not proof of customer demand, procurement eligibility, funding approval, or government endorsement.

## 2. ACT Development Context

### 2.1 ACT Government Technology Directions

The ACT Government Technology Directions provide guidance for technology investment decisions by ACT Government directorates.

The published directions emphasize:

- Reusing fit-for-purpose technology before investing in new solutions.
- Consolidating legacy systems onto endorsed platforms where appropriate.
- Developing scalable whole-of-government solutions in stages.
- Considering security, privacy, and data management.
- Supporting value for money and better community services.

Official source:
https://www.act.gov.au/open/act-government-technology-directions

**Potential QAI / FAEP relevance:** interoperability, platform integration, workflow orchestration, data governance, lifecycle management, staged implementation, and reuse of existing systems.

These are potential design alignments. Each proposed solution must be assessed against the actual requirements of the relevant directorate and project.

### 2.2 CBR2030 — Strategic Economic Development Framework

CBR2030 sets out four missions:

1. A city that continues to give you back time.
2. A climate-ready city, leading Australia's net zero future.
3. Continued knowledge-based economic growth.
4. Building Canberra's future today.

Official source:
https://www.act.gov.au/open/cbr2030-acts-strategic-economic-development-framework

Potential investigation areas include digital public services, productivity, knowledge-based industries, climate and resource management, and enabling infrastructure.

These missions provide context for opportunity discovery; they do not establish demand for a specific product or service.

## 3. Digital Government and Architecture

For an ACT Government opportunity, determine which technology, architecture, and delivery requirements apply.

| Area | Initial assessment |
|---|---|
| Reuse and consolidation | Identify existing endorsed platforms and systems before proposing replacements. |
| Whole-of-government capability | Assess whether an existing shared solution can meet the requirement or whether a new capability is justified. |
| Digital service delivery | Consider end-to-end digital workflows, accessibility, and community needs. |
| Identity and access | Identify applicable identity, authentication, authorization, and federation requirements. |
| Data governance | Assess ownership, quality, permitted use, retention, sharing, and protection. |
| Security and privacy | Identify applicable risk assessments, controls, approvals, and assurance evidence. |
| Integration | Map interfaces, dependencies, service boundaries, and interoperability requirements. |
| Operations | Define support, monitoring, recovery, maintenance, and lifecycle responsibilities. |
| Value for money | Assess whole-of-life cost, benefits, reuse options, and delivery risk. |

The ACT Government Technology Directions should be reviewed alongside project-specific requirements and the relevant directorate's instructions.

## 4. Procurement and Supplier Readiness

ACT Government procurement is governed by a framework that includes legislation, legislative instruments, policies, guidance, tools, templates, and systems.

Official starting points:

- Procurement ACT: https://www.procurement.act.gov.au/
- Supplying to ACT Government: https://www.procurement.act.gov.au/supplying-to-act-government
- Procurement policies and guidance: https://www.procurement.act.gov.au/policy-and-resources/policies-and-guidance
- Finding future opportunities: https://www.procurement.act.gov.au/supplying-to-act-government/find-opportunities-for-your-business/future-opportunities
- Step-by-step supplier guide: https://www.procurement.act.gov.au/supplying-to-act-government/getting-ready-to-work-with-the-act-government/step-by-step-guide-to-supplying-to-ACT-Government

Initial activities:

1. Review supplier registration and business-information requirements.
2. Identify the relevant tender and quotation channels.
3. Review current procurement legislation and thresholds.
4. Identify any applicable supplier prequalification, certification, or panel requirements.
5. Understand tender response formats, closing dates, and mandatory evidence.
6. Assess contract terms, insurance, delivery capacity, and support obligations.
7. Record each opportunity and its decision deadline in the procurement register.

Procurement rules and thresholds can change. Confirm the current rules for the specific procurement before deciding how to participate.

## 5. ACT Procurement Considerations

The ACT Government's published procurement guidance describes different sourcing methods according to procurement value and type, and identifies value for money as an important requirement.

The Procurement ACT website also provides information on supplier registration, future opportunities, standing offers, panels, multi-use lists, and unsolicited proposals.

A supplier's registration or panel membership does not by itself guarantee an award or future work.

For each opportunity, record:

- Procurement reference and issuing entity.
- Published scope and expected deliverables.
- Estimated value, if officially disclosed.
- Eligibility and participation conditions.
- Required certifications, evidence, and supplier declarations.
- Closing date and submission method.
- Applicable evaluation criteria.
- Questions, clarifications, and addenda.
- Decision, outcome, and follow-up action.

Official source:
https://www.procurement.act.gov.au/supplying-to-act-government/getting-ready-to-work-with-the-act-government/ways-to-supply

## 7. QAI / FAEP Alignment Method

Use the following sequence:

**ACT Source → Stakeholder Need → Defined Problem → Capability Mapping → Requirements Assessment → Evidence → Pilot → Commercial Decision**

1. Record the official source or published opportunity.
2. Define the operational or community problem.
3. Identify the responsible agency, customer, user, or operator.
4. Map candidate QAI / FAEP capabilities to the problem.
5. Identify applicable security, privacy, architecture, procurement, and contractual requirements.
6. Assess readiness and evidence gaps.
7. Define a bounded pilot with measurable acceptance criteria.
8. Evaluate costs, support, integration, delivery, and commercial viability.

Apply the QAI / FAEP implementation principle where appropriate:

**Reuse → Connect → Augment → Automate → Optimize → Innovate**

This principle is a proposed development approach, not a claim that the ACT Government requires this exact sequence.

## 8. Opportunity Maturity

Use evidence-based stages to prevent a general policy match from being mistaken for a validated opportunity.

| Stage | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|
| Policy-aligned | Relevant official source and documented objective. |
| Problem hypothesis | Specific problem and potential stakeholder. |
| Stakeholder-validated | Evidence that the relevant stakeholder confirms the problem. |
| Technically assessed | Feasibility, integration, dependencies, readiness, and gaps documented. |
| Pilot-ready | Scope, resources, controls, baseline, and acceptance criteria defined. |
| Demonstrated | Test or pilot results recorded. |
| Commercially assessed | Delivery model, cost, support, procurement or sales route, and viability assessed. |

## 9. Evidence and Compliance

For each material requirement or opportunity, record:

- Source title, URL, issuing organization, and date.
- Relevant provision and scope.
- Jurisdiction and applicability rationale.
- Requirement classification: mandatory, contractual, recommended, voluntary/adopted, opportunity signal, or unverified.
- Related QAI / FAEP capability.
- Current implementation status and evidence location.
- Technical, operational, security, privacy, and procurement gaps.
- Owner, next action, and review date.

Keep policy alignment separate from stakeholder validation, implementation evidence, procurement eligibility, and commercial assumptions.
