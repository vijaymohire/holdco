## 1. Objectives

This directory provides a structured starting point for assessing how QAI / FAEP capabilities could address validated needs in NSW government, industry, and regional ecosystems.

It supports:

- Understanding NSW industry missions and focus sectors.
- Identifying potential technology, modernization, research, and service opportunities.
- Understanding relevant digital-government, AI, cloud, cybersecurity, and procurement requirements.
- Mapping candidate QAI / FAEP capabilities to specific problems and stakeholder needs.
- Assessing technical readiness, integration requirements, assurance, and evidence gaps.
- Identifying potential pilots, partnerships, tenders, and commercialization pathways.
- Recording source documents, applicability, follow-up actions, and review dates.

**Important:** Alignment with an NSW priority is an opportunity signal. It does not establish customer demand, procurement eligibility, funding eligibility, government endorsement, or a commitment to purchase.

## 2. NSW Industry Missions

The NSW Industry Policy identifies three connected missions:

1. **Housing** — supporting safe, secure, affordable, well-designed, and sustainable housing.
2. **Net zero and energy transition** — supporting a competitive, sustainable, low-carbon economy.
3. **Local manufacturing** — strengthening productivity, innovation, supply chains, and sovereign industrial capability.

Official source:
https://www.nsw.gov.au/departments-and-agencies/investment-nsw/resources/nsw-industry-policy

Potential QAI / FAEP investigation areas include digital engineering, asset and project monitoring, energy optimization, industrial automation, digital twins, data integration, and production workflows. These are proposed application areas, not confirmed government requirements or commissioned projects.

## 4. NSW Digital-Government Context

For government-facing digital work, review the applicable NSW requirements before selecting the architecture or implementation approach.

| Area | Initial assessment |
|---|---|
| Digital policy | Identify applicable whole-of-government and agency digital requirements. |
| AI governance | Determine whether AI use-case registration, risk assessment, assurance, or additional review is required. |
| Cloud | Assess cloud and non-cloud options under the current policy, including security, cost, service quality, and deployment suitability. |
| Cybersecurity | Identify required security controls, risk assessments, incident processes, and assurance evidence. |
| Privacy and data | Assess personal information, sensitive data, data-sharing permissions, retention, and permitted use. |
| Architecture and integration | Identify approved interfaces, identity requirements, interoperability constraints, and existing platforms. |
| Procurement | Determine the purchasing pathway, supplier conditions, contract requirements, and applicable procurement rules. |
| Assurance | Establish project approvals, testing, acceptance criteria, and evidence retention. |

Official starting points:

- NSW Digital Policy: https://digital.nsw.gov.au/policy
- NSW AI Assessment Framework: https://www.digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/artificial-intelligence/ai-governance-assurance-and-frameworks/nsw-ai-assessment-framework
- NSW Cloud Policy: https://digital.nsw.gov.au/policy/cloud-policy
- NSW Procurement Policy Framework: https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/policy-library/policies/procurement-policy-framework
- NSW ICT Purchasing Framework: https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/resources/ICT-Purchasing-Framework

Verify current versions, effective dates, applicability, and relevant agency directions before relying on a requirement.

## 5. Procurement and Commercialization

Potential pathways include:

- Responding to published tenders and requests for quotation.
- Working as a subcontractor or technology partner.
- Partnering with NSW-based technology businesses, manufacturers, research organizations, and service providers.
- Proposing a bounded pilot where an appropriate sponsor and pathway exist.
- Exploring industry development and investment support programs.
- Assessing the NSW unsolicited proposals process where a proposal meets its published requirements.

Relevant sources:

- NSW procurement guidance: https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/
- NSW ICT Purchasing Framework: https://www.info.buy.nsw.gov.au/resources/ICT-Purchasing-Framework
- NSW unsolicited proposals: https://www.nsw.gov.au/nsw-government/engage-us/unsolicited-proposals

An unsolicited proposal is not a substitute for checking the published process, uniqueness criteria, value-for-money considerations, public benefit, and pre-lodgement requirements. Nor does it guarantee that government will negotiate or award a contract.

## 6. QAI / FAEP Alignment Method

Use the following sequence for each NSW opportunity:

**Source → Need → Stakeholder → Capability → Requirements → Evidence → Pilot → Commercial Decision**

1. Record the official source and its stated objective.
2. Define the problem without assuming that a particular QAI product is required.
3. Identify the relevant user, buyer, operator, and other affected stakeholders.
4. Map candidate QAI / FAEP capabilities to the problem.
5. Determine applicable legal, contractual, procurement, security, privacy, and assurance requirements.
6. Assess current implementation readiness and identify missing evidence.
7. Define a bounded pilot with a baseline and measurable acceptance criteria.
8. Evaluate delivery feasibility, operating costs, support, procurement, and commercialization options.

Apply the QAI / FAEP implementation principle where appropriate:

**Reuse → Connect → Augment → Automate → Optimize → Innovate**

## 7. NSW Opportunity Status

Classify each opportunity using evidence-based stages:

- **Policy-aligned:** An authoritative source supports the general area of interest.
- **Problem hypothesis:** A specific problem and potential user have been identified.
- **Stakeholder-validated:** Relevant stakeholders confirm the problem and its importance.
- **Technically assessed:** Feasibility, integration, readiness, and gaps have been assessed.
- **Pilot-ready:** Scope, resources, controls, baseline, and acceptance criteria are defined.
- **Demonstrated:** Results are recorded from a test or pilot.
- **Commercially assessed:** Delivery, support, costs, procurement or sales route, and viability have been evaluated.

Do not mark an opportunity as validated merely because it aligns with a policy or focus sector.

## 10. Evidence and Review Requirements

For each material claim or opportunity, record:

- Source title, URL, issuing organization, and jurisdiction.
- Publication or effective date, where available.
- Relevant provision and applicability rationale.
- Requirement classification: mandatory, contractual, recommended, voluntary/adopted, opportunity signal, or unverified.
- Stakeholder and problem statement.
- Related QAI / FAEP capability.
- Current readiness and evidence location.
- Gap, risk, owner, next action, and review date.

Keep policy evidence separate from stakeholder evidence, implementation evidence, procurement eligibility, and commercial assumptions.

Review source currency before submitting a tender, proposal, or project plan.
