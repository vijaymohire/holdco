## Release Update — 3 October 2026: National Outcomes, Sector Problems and QAI Opportunities

### 1. Purpose

This document connects Australian national priorities to industry sectors, potential problem statements, target users, QAI product opportunities and measurable outcomes.

It provides a bridge between:

- Australian national and industrial priorities.
- Sector-specific challenges and operational needs.
- Reusable QAI platform products and capabilities.
- Country-specific configurations and integrations.
- Current-pilot demonstrations and post-pilot opportunities.
- Research, commercialization, investment and potential market adoption.

The goal is to identify where QAI products could help address real problems while keeping development focused, measurable and commercially relevant.

This is a strategic opportunity map. Sector problems require stakeholder validation, and proposed product opportunities are not evidence of confirmed demand, technical feasibility, government endorsement or funding eligibility.

### 2. National Outcomes Map

Use this map to connect broad national objectives to potential sector-level contributions and QAI capabilities.

| National outcome area | Sector-level objective | Potential QAI contribution | Measures to investigate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Productivity and competitiveness | Improve operating efficiency and decision quality. | AI-enabled workflows, algorithms, resource coordination and digital twins. | Time per task, throughput, cost per unit, error rate and utilization. |
| Domestic industrial capability | Develop reusable engineering and technology capabilities. | Shared QAI platforms, modular products, local integrations and engineering toolkits. | Reusable components, local supplier participation, skills developed and deployment effort. |
| Research and commercialization | Translate research into validated applications. | QAI Research Hub, QAI PoC Lab, experiment management and product development tools. | Reproducibility, validated use cases, partner engagement and progression to deployment. |
| Resilient supply chains | Improve visibility, planning and response to disruptions. | Data integration, scenario modelling, optimization and workflow coordination. | Lead time, service levels, recovery time and dependency visibility. |
| Energy and resource efficiency | Improve the use of energy, water, materials and other resources. | Monitoring, simulation, analytics and decision support. | Resource consumption per unit, cost, waste and operational constraints. |
| Responsible AI and automation | Support safe, controlled and accountable adoption. | Runtime controls, access management, traceability, evaluation and human approval. | Test coverage, traceability, exception rates and adherence to defined controls. |
| Regional development | Support practical technology adoption across regional and remote industries. | Modular deployment, edge/cloud integration, remote monitoring and reusable pilot configurations. | Deployment cost, service availability, travel avoided and local capability development. |
| Critical technology capability | Build and validate capabilities in AI, quantum, advanced ICT and related fields. | QAI Algorithms, QAI QPU integration, QAI Runtime and hybrid experimentation. | Reproducibility, execution cost, solution quality and comparative performance. |

The measures above are candidate KPIs. Each project must select metrics appropriate to its use case and establish a baseline before making improvement claims.

### 4. Problem-to-Product Map

This map identifies common problem patterns that may recur across multiple sectors. Reusing a common capability can be more effective than building a separate product for every industry.

| Problem pattern | Typical symptoms to investigate | Shared QAI capability | Possible sector applications |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fragmented data and assets | Information is distributed across incompatible systems. | QAI Hub, data/interface integration and Digital Twin components. | Agriculture, manufacturing, utilities and infrastructure. |
| Manual or repetitive workflows | Staff repeatedly collect, transform, check or transfer information. | QAI Agent Framework, QAI Runtime and workflow automation. | Research, administration, operations and engineering. |
| Resource allocation | Decisions must account for multiple constraints and competing objectives. | QAI Algorithms, optimization interfaces and resource management. | Energy, agriculture, logistics and manufacturing. |
| Complex scenario evaluation | Operational alternatives are costly or difficult to test in the real environment. | Simulation, emulation, experiment management and Digital Twins. | Agriculture, industrial operations, infrastructure and research. |
| Inconsistent execution | Workloads behave differently across environments or deployments. | QAI OS, QAI Runtime, QAI Cloud and deployment toolkits. | Research computing, enterprise IT and distributed industry. |
| Limited operational visibility | Users cannot easily see system state, execution status or exceptions. | QAI Hub, observability, telemetry and appropriate user interfaces. | Data centres, manufacturing, utilities and Digital Farm. |
| Difficult validation | Results cannot be easily reproduced or compared with a baseline. | QAI Research Hub, QAI PoC Lab and experiment records. | All research and product-development sectors. |
| Integration and interoperability | Existing systems have different interfaces, data structures or access rules. | QAI Routers, QAI Hub, runtime adapters and documented APIs. | Enterprise IT, operational technology and partner ecosystems. |
| Limited computing flexibility | Workloads have different hardware, latency, cost or deployment requirements. | QAI Datacenter, QAI OS, QAI Runtime and QAI Cloud. | Research, AI infrastructure and selected industrial workloads. |
| Technology adoption risk | Organizations lack sufficient evidence to adopt a new technology. | QAI PoC Lab, ProductDev Toolkit, evaluation frameworks and staged pilots. | Manufacturing, agriculture, research and public-sector innovation. |

| QAI QPU | Access to or integration with supported quantum-processing resources. | Quantum research, hybrid experiments and selected optimization studies. |
| QAI OS | System-level abstractions and services for supported environments. | Research platforms, controlled deployments and industrial systems. |
| QAI Algorithms | Reusable algorithms for defined computational problems. | Farm planning, resource optimization, scheduling and scenario analysis. |
| QAI Routers | Routing or selection across supported endpoints, workloads or execution paths. | Distributed computing, hybrid execution and service integration. |
| QAI Runtime | Execution of supported workloads and runtime coordination. | AI workflows, simulation, experiments and industrial applications. |
| QAI Cloud | Cloud-based access, integration or deployment within the implemented scope. | Research environments, remote pilots and distributed applications. |
| QAI Hub | Coordination and access across selected products, services and workflows. | Digital Farm, multi-asset environments and partner integrations. |
| QAI Agent Framework | Agent-based task execution and coordination where implemented. | Engineering assistance, workflow support and bounded decision-support tasks. |
| QAI Research Hub | Research organization, experiment records and reusable knowledge. | University collaboration, applied research and product validation. |
| QAI PoC Lab | Controlled demonstrations and technical proof-of-concept work. | Digital Farm, industrial pilots and partner evaluations. |
| QAI ProductDev Toolkit | Reusable development processes and engineering tools. | Product families and sector-specific configurations. |
| QAI ProductDeploy Toolkit | Packaging, configuration and deployment support. | Repeatable pilot deployments and partner environments. |

| QAI BareMetal Framework | Provisioning and management for supported bare-metal or restricted-network environments. | Specialized infrastructure and controlled deployment environments. |

A product should be mapped to a sector only when its capabilities and dependencies are relevant to the actual problem. Product availability and readiness must be checked against repository evidence.

### 6. Priority Map for the Initial Digital Farm Pilot

The Digital Farm is the initial application through which selected shared capabilities can be demonstrated.

| Pilot function | Problem being explored | Product families involved | Evidence of progress |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asset inventory | Establish a structured view of the selected farm assets. | QAI Hub, data structures and Digital Twin components. | Verified asset inventory and schemas. |
| Asset virtualization | Represent selected assets in a virtual environment. | Digital Twin components, QAI OS or runtime services where required. | Working virtual asset and repeatable instantiation. |
| Data and scenario inputs | Supply consistent inputs for controlled experiments. | QAI PoC Lab, data and experiment tooling. | Versioned inputs, schemas and validation results. |
| Workflow execution | Connect data, processing and scenario stages. | QAI Runtime, QAI Hub and QAI Algorithms. | Repeatable execution trace and outputs. |
| Simulation and feedback | Evaluate selected scenarios and closed-loop behavior. | Simulation, emulation and workflow components. | Scenario results and state-transition records. |
| Classical baseline | Establish a conventional reference result. | QAI Algorithms and experiment tooling. | Baseline method, assumptions and metrics. |
| QAI/hybrid experiment | Evaluate only the QAI or hybrid methods actually implemented. | QAI Algorithms, QAI Runtime and QAI QPU integration if available. | Reproducible experiment and comparison evidence. |
| KPI and value evaluation | Assess technical and operational outcomes. | QAI Research Hub and QAI PoC Lab. | KPI definitions, baseline and measured results. |
| Demonstration and handover | Make the pilot understandable and repeatable. | QAI PoC Lab and engineering toolkits. | Instructions, outputs, limitations and evidence package. |

The pilot should first demonstrate the capabilities already available. Additional GUI workflows and complex orchestration can follow once the underlying operations, interfaces and acceptance criteria have been validated.

### 7. Post-Pilot Sector Expansion Map

Expansion should be based on reusable assets and validated sector needs rather than a requirement to develop every sector in parallel.

| Expansion stage | Candidate work | Required evidence or decision |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1 — Current pilot | Demonstrate the bounded Digital Farm workflow. | Repeatable execution, baseline and agreed pilot acceptance evidence. |
| Stage 2 — Pilot completion | Close essential gaps and document reusable components. | Gap closure, test results and known limitations. |
| Stage 3 — Second use case | Adapt selected components to one additional use case or sector. | Stakeholder-validated problem and documented adaptation effort. |
| Stage 4 — Reusable productization | Separate common services from application-specific configuration. | Stable interfaces, tests, packaging and support requirements. |
| Stage 5 — Partner integration | Connect the selected products to partner systems and operating processes. | Agreed integration scope, security requirements and acceptance criteria. |
| Stage 6 — Advanced orchestration and GUI | Add user-facing workflow composition, approvals and dashboards where needed. | Defined user tasks, stable APIs and evidence that the interface adds value. |
| Stage 7 — Commercial deployment | Establish delivery, support, licensing and operational arrangements. | Customer validation, commercial assumptions and agreed responsibilities. |
| Stage 8 — Multi-sector ecosystem | Reuse products across additional sectors and regional ecosystems. | Evidence of transferability, sector-specific validation and sustainable delivery. |

These stages are a planning model, not fixed dates or a commitment to implement every opportunity.

### 8. Target User and Stakeholder Map

Separate the people who use a product from those who sponsor, operate, integrate, regulate or benefit from it.

| Stakeholder group | Primary concern | Potential engagement or demonstration |
|---|---|---|
| Industry operator | Practical operational benefit and reliability. | Demonstrate a bounded workflow using representative data and measurable outcomes. |
| Technical or engineering team | Integration, maintainability, performance and system boundaries. | Review architecture, APIs, deployment steps and test evidence. |
| Business owner or executive | Business value, risk, cost and delivery commitments. | Present baseline, use case, expected value and unresolved assumptions. |
| Research organization | Technical validity, reproducibility and research opportunities. | Share an appropriately scoped experiment plan and evidence package. |
| Technology integrator | Compatibility with existing systems and delivery responsibilities. | Review interfaces, dependencies and integration requirements. |
| Investor or commercialization partner | IP boundaries, market need, development cost and commercialization pathway. | Present selected product evidence and a staged investment proposal. |
| Government or public-sector organization | Public value, procurement fit, assurance and compliance. | Map a validated use case to published priorities and applicable procurement requirements. |
| Workforce and training partner | Skills development, usability and adoption. | Demonstrate the tools and define training needs. |
| Community or regional organization | Local relevance, accessibility and community outcomes. | Co-design an appropriate use case and define the measures that matter locally. |

Do not treat a stakeholder category as a confirmed customer or assume that a policy priority represents an approved procurement requirement.

### 9. Opportunity Assessment and Validation

For each sector opportunity, create a separate record.

Required fields:

- Opportunity ID and sector.
- National priority and official source.
- Problem statement and evidence source.
- Affected users and stakeholders.
- Current workflow or existing solution.
- Frequency, impact and cost of the problem, where known.
- Proposed QAI product configuration.
- Existing assets that can be reused.
- Missing capabilities and external integrations.
- Classical baseline or incumbent alternative.
- Technical, operational, security and regulatory constraints.
- Candidate KPIs and acceptance criteria.
- Potential deployment and support model.
- IP and licensing considerations.
- Partner or customer validation status.
- Next action and review date.

Use the following opportunity statuses:

- **Policy-aligned:** related to a documented national priority.
- **Problem hypothesis:** plausible problem requiring stakeholder validation.
- **Stakeholder-validated:** a relevant stakeholder has confirmed the problem.
- **Technically assessed:** an initial feasibility assessment has been completed.
- **Pilot-ready:** scope, data, resources and acceptance criteria are sufficiently defined.
- **Demonstrated:** a repeatable demonstration has been completed.
- **Commercially assessed:** delivery costs, value assumptions and a potential commercial pathway have been evaluated.

These statuses describe evidence progression, not an overall score or guarantee of success.

### 10. Mapping Method

Use the following chain for each candidate opportunity:

**National priority → Sector → Problem → Target user → Current baseline → QAI product opportunity → Integration requirements → Pilot → Measured result → Commercialization decision**

For every proposed product opportunity, answer:

1. What documented national or sector priority does it relate to?
2. What specific problem is being addressed?
3. Who experiences the problem, and who would use or fund the solution?
4. What evidence establishes the problem and its significance?
5. Which existing QAI products can be reused?
6. What functionality is available now, and what still needs to be built?
7. What existing solution provides the comparison baseline?
8. What result would justify further investment?
9. What data, approvals, partnerships or integrations are required?
10. How would the resulting product be delivered, supported and licensed?

If these questions cannot yet be answered, record the uncertainty and identify the next evidence-gathering action.

### Release Summary

This release establishes a multi-map framework connecting Australian national outcomes, sectors, problems, target users, shared QAI products and measurable results.

The intended progression is:

**Understand national outcomes → Validate sector problems → Reuse shared QAI products → Demonstrate a bounded pilot → Measure results → Productize reusable capabilities → Expand through validated partners and sectors.**

The Digital Farm remains the initial demonstrator. Broader QAI Datacenter, QAI QPU, QAI OS, QAI Algorithms, QAI Routers, QAI Runtime, QAI Cloud and QAI Hub development should be connected to evidence, defined product boundaries and validated application needs.
