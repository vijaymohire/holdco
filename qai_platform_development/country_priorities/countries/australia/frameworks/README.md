
# Australia Frameworks — README

**Status:** In Progress
**Purpose:** Establish a common framework for assessing Australian government priorities, jurisdictional requirements, technology standards, procurement pathways, and commercialization opportunities relevant to the QAI / FAEP ecosystem.

**Last reviewed:** 3 October 2026

---

## 1. Objectives

This directory provides a structured method to:

- Identify relevant Australian Government, state and territory, and industry requirements.
- Understand the relationship between policy objectives, public needs, technical capabilities, and project delivery.
- Map QAI / FAEP products, services, modernization offerings, and research capabilities to relevant requirements.
- Distinguish mandatory obligations from contractual conditions, recommended practices, and voluntary alignment.
- Record capability readiness, supporting evidence, identified gaps, and follow-up actions.
- Support government procurement, private-sector commercialization, and pilot development without assuming eligibility or endorsement.

## 3. Framework Operating Model

Use the following sequence when evaluating a new opportunity:

**Identify → Understand → Map → Assess → Validate → Demonstrate → Commercialize**

1. **Identify:** Find the relevant official source, procurement notice, stakeholder need, or industry opportunity.
2. **Understand:** Record its scope, jurisdiction, intended audience, applicability, and relevant dates.
3. **Map:** Identify potentially relevant QAI / FAEP capabilities and existing systems.
4. **Assess:** Review technical readiness, compliance implications, dependencies, costs, and capability gaps.
5. **Validate:** Confirm the need and requirements with authoritative sources, customers, or relevant stakeholders.
6. **Demonstrate:** Test the proposed solution against measurable acceptance criteria.
7. **Commercialize:** Assess procurement, delivery, partnership, production, support, and financial viability.

Apply the QAI / FAEP implementation principle where appropriate:

**Reuse → Connect → Augment → Automate → Optimize → Innovate**

## 4. Requirement Classification

Classify each recorded requirement using one of the following categories:

- **Mandatory:** An applicable legal, regulatory, or formally binding obligation.
- **Contractual:** A condition imposed through a contract, tender, funding agreement, or other binding arrangement.
- **Recommended:** Official guidance or a recommended practice that is not independently binding.
- **Voluntary / Adopted:** A standard, framework, or control voluntarily adopted by the organization.
- **Opportunity Signal:** A policy priority, strategic objective, or market indicator that may warrant further investigation.
- **Unverified:** A requirement or interpretation that still needs confirmation.

Applicability must be assessed in context. A government policy does not automatically apply to every private-sector project. Record the basis for any claim that a requirement applies.

## 5. Capability and Evidence Classification

Do not treat a policy match or product description as proof of implementation.

Use the following capability states when mapping QAI / FAEP offerings:

- Planned
- Designed / Documented
- Partially Implemented
- Implemented — Verification Pending
- Demonstrated
- Validated Against Acceptance Criteria
- Not Assessed
- Not Applicable

Where possible, associate each readiness claim with a repository location, test result, demonstration record, architecture document, assessment, or other supporting evidence.

## 7. Source and Evidence Rules

For each material requirement or opportunity, record:

- Source title and authoritative URL.
- Issuing organization and jurisdiction.
- Publication, effective, review, or closing date, where available.
- Relevant provision or source passage.
- Requirement classification and applicability rationale.
- Related QAI / FAEP capability or product.
- Current readiness and evidence location.
- Gap, risk, owner, and next action.
- Date last checked.

Prefer official legislation, government policy, procurement documentation, and standards-body publications for authoritative claims. Use secondary sources for context, not as substitutes for binding source documents.

Distinguish **policy alignment, stakeholder-validated demand, technical feasibility, demonstrated capability, procurement eligibility, funding eligibility, and commercial viability**. These are separate assessment outcomes.
