# Policy to Capability Mapping

**Status:** In Progress
**Owner:** Bhadale IT / QAI-FAEP
**Jurisdiction:** Australia — Commonwealth, New South Wales (NSW), Australian Capital Territory (ACT)
**Purpose:** Policy alignment, capability traceability, evidence management and opportunity assessment
**Last reviewed:** 3 October 2026

---

## 1. Purpose

This document defines a consistent method for mapping Australian government priorities, public needs, technology requirements, procurement conditions and commercialization considerations to relevant QAI/FAEP capabilities.

The mapping is intended to support:

* Australian market and policy research.
* QAI/FAEP capability development and validation.
* Government and private-sector project planning.
* Pilot identification and readiness assessment.
* Procurement opportunity screening.
* Evidence-based proposal development.
* Responsible commercialization and intellectual property protection.

This document establishes a traceability method. It does not imply government endorsement, procurement eligibility, funding eligibility, technical suitability or a commitment to purchase.

## 2. Scope

The mapping covers five requirement categories:

1. **Policy and strategic priorities:** national industry development, digital transformation, critical technologies, productivity, innovation and economic development.
2. **Digital and technical requirements:** architecture, cloud, AI, cybersecurity, data governance, interoperability, automation and quantum technologies.
3. **Public and industry needs:** validated operational problems, service improvements, resilience, productivity and measurable outcomes.
4. **Procurement and project requirements:** supplier eligibility, contractual conditions, assurance, delivery capability, evaluation criteria and reporting.
5. **Commercialization requirements:** intellectual property, local-value evidence, Australian manufacturing claims where applicable, partnerships and routes to market.

### 2.1 Jurisdictional coverage

| Jurisdiction         | Mapping focus                                                                                      |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Commonwealth         | National priorities, industry policy, critical technologies and relevant national programs         |
| NSW                  | Digital government, AI assurance, cloud, cybersecurity, quantum-related policy and ICT procurement |
| ACT                  | Technology investment, digital services, data governance and procurement                           |
| Cross-jurisdictional | Common engineering, security, assurance, interoperability and commercialization requirements       |
| Private sector       | Buyer-defined business requirements, contracts, technical acceptance and commercial outcomes       |

## 3. Mapping principles

1. **Authoritative sources first.** Prefer official legislation, policies, procurement directions, technical standards and program documentation.
2. **Traceability.** Each material requirement must have a source, a defined scope and a unique identifier.
3. **Evidence before claims.** A documented capability is not automatically an implemented, demonstrated or validated capability.
4. **Separation of concerns.** Distinguish policy intent, public need, technical requirements, procurement conditions and commercial objectives.
5. **Jurisdictional configuration.** Reuse the common QAI/FAEP core while recording jurisdiction-specific controls and obligations.
6. **Proportionate assurance.** Determine applicable assurance activities from the actual use case, data, risks, contract and governing requirements.
7. **IP protection.** Disclose only the technical and commercial information needed for the relevant assessment or engagement.
8. **Continuous review.** Recheck source currency, applicability and implementation status before relying on a mapping in a proposal or bid.

## 4. End-to-end traceability model

The standard mapping chain is:

**Authoritative Source → Policy or Requirement → Public/Buyer Need → Required Outcome → QAI/FAEP Capability → Implementation Status → Evidence → Gap → Action → Acceptance Criterion**

Each link must be recorded explicitly. Where evidence is missing, the mapping must identify the gap rather than infer that the requirement has been satisfied.

### 4.1 Required mapping record

| Field                   | Description                                                           |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mapping ID              | Unique identifier, such as `AU-NSW-AI-001`                            |
| Requirement title       | Concise name of the policy, need or requirement                       |
| Requirement category    | Policy, technical, public need, procurement or commercialization      |
| Jurisdiction            | Commonwealth, NSW, ACT, cross-jurisdictional or private sector        |
| Source authority        | Publishing agency or contracting entity                               |
| Source URL              | Direct link to the authoritative source                               |
| Source date             | Publication, issue or update date, where available                    |
| Date verified           | Date the source was checked                                           |
| Applicability           | Mandatory, conditional, advisory, strategic context or not applicable |
| Requirement summary     | Concise description supported by the source                           |
| Intended outcome        | Required public, operational or commercial result                     |
| QAI/FAEP capability     | Relevant product, framework, service or engineering capability        |
| Capability relationship | Direct, enabling, supporting, exploratory or not established          |
| Readiness status        | Status selected from Section 6                                        |
| Evidence references     | Repository paths, tests, demonstrations, reports or external records  |
| Evidence limitations    | Missing tests, unverified claims, scope restrictions or assumptions   |
| Gap                     | Unmet requirement or missing evidence                                 |
| Follow-up action        | Concrete work required to close the gap                               |
| Acceptance criterion    | Observable condition used to assess completion                        |
| Owner                   | Responsible person or team                                            |
| Review date             | Next planned review                                                   |
| Decision                | Proceed, investigate, remediate, defer or exclude                     |

### 5.4 Source verification rules

For each source:

* Record the issuing authority and direct URL.
* Capture the publication or revision date when available.
* Identify the intended audience and jurisdiction.
* Distinguish mandatory requirements from recommendations and strategic aspirations.
* Record any threshold, exemption, commencement date or conditional applicability.
* Recheck the source before relying on it for a live opportunity.

If a source cannot be verified, mark it **Unverified** and do not use it as evidence of compliance.

## 6. Capability readiness and evidence classification

Use the following controlled vocabulary consistently across the Australian country-priorities repository.

| Status                             | Meaning                                                               | Minimum supporting basis                               |
| ---------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Planned                            | Intended work not yet sufficiently specified or implemented           | Recorded scope or plan                                 |
| Designed/Documented                | Architecture, design or process is documented                         | Reviewable design artefact                             |
| Partially Implemented              | Some scoped components exist                                          | Code or implementation records identifying limitations |
| Implemented — Verification Pending | Implementation exists but has not completed the required verification | Implementation reference and outstanding test list     |
| Demonstrated                       | The defined capability has been shown working in a recorded scenario  | Reproducible demonstration and results                 |
| Validated                          | Defined acceptance criteria have been met with recorded evidence      | Test or validation report against criteria             |
| Not Assessed                       | Readiness has not yet been evaluated                                  | Assessment task recorded                               |
| Not Applicable                     | The capability or requirement does not apply to the defined scope     | Documented rationale                                   |

A status applies only to the specified capability, version, environment and use case. Demonstration in a simulated environment does not establish production readiness or operational suitability.

### 6.1 Evidence categories

* **E1 — Documentary:** architecture, specifications, requirements, policies and design documents.
* **E2 — Implementation:** source code, configuration, build records and versioned artefacts.
* **E3 — Test:** test cases, execution logs, expected-versus-actual results and defect records.
* **E4 — Demonstration:** recorded scenarios, demonstrations, sample inputs and outputs.
* **E5 — Validation:** acceptance reports, measured outcomes and independent or customer verification where applicable.
* **E6 — Commercial/organizational:** supplier credentials, contracts, IP ownership records, Australian operations or manufacturing evidence, as relevant.

Evidence must be linked to its actual repository location or authoritative record. Do not assign an evidence category merely because a document claims a capability exists.

### 7.1 Rules for mapping quantum capabilities

Quantum-related policy references must not be treated as proof that a proposed workload offers quantum advantage.

For each candidate workload, record:

1. The problem and classical baseline.
2. The proposed quantum or hybrid method.
3. Whether the work uses simulation, emulation, virtualization or physical quantum hardware.
4. The metrics used to compare performance, cost, accuracy and resource consumption.
5. The experimental results and their limitations.
6. The criteria that would justify further quantum-specific investment.

## 8. Gap and action management

Each material gap must have a unique identifier and a clear closure condition.

| Field                | Example                                                                       |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Gap ID               | `GAP-AU-001`                                                                  |
| Related mapping      | `AU-NSW-AI-001`                                                               |
| Gap description      | Evidence of lifecycle risk assessment is incomplete                           |
| Risk                 | Assurance requirements may not be demonstrable                                |
| Required action      | Define the assessment workflow and produce a sample evidence record           |
| Acceptance criterion | The sample record covers the applicable lifecycle stages and identified risks |
| Priority             | Critical, High, Medium or Low, with rationale                                 |
| Owner                | Assigned responsible person or team                                           |
| Status               | Open, In Progress, Blocked, Ready for Review or Closed                        |
| Closure evidence     | Link to the completed artefact or test result                                 |

Prioritisation should consider actual requirement applicability, safety and security risk, project dependencies, delivery effort and commercial relevance.

## 9. Jurisdictional configuration

The mapping will feed into the QAI/FAEP jurisdictional configuration model.

Maintain:

* A **common core** for architecture, engineering, lifecycle management, security and evidence handling.
* A **Commonwealth configuration** for applicable national policies, programs and procurement conditions.
* A **NSW configuration** for relevant digital, AI, cloud, cybersecurity and procurement requirements.
* An **ACT configuration** for relevant technology investment, digital, data and procurement requirements.
* A **project-specific configuration** for buyer requirements, data classification, contracts, assurance and acceptance.

Do not assume that satisfying one jurisdiction's requirements establishes compliance with another jurisdiction's rules.

## 10. Government and private-sector lifecycle separation

The mapping must distinguish government procurement obligations from private buyer requirements.

### Government projects

Assess the applicable procurement route, supplier eligibility, evaluation criteria, probity, security, privacy, assurance, contractual conditions and reporting obligations.

### Private-sector projects

Assess buyer-defined needs, technical requirements, commercial terms, data and security controls, service levels, acceptance criteria, integration constraints and business outcomes.

Both lifecycle models can reuse common QAI/FAEP engineering and governance components. Project-specific legal and contractual requirements remain separately traceable.

## 11. Pilot selection

Use the mapping to identify pilot candidates where existing evidence is strongest.

The initial candidate is the **Digital Farm CPS/Digital Twin**.

Assess it against:

* A clearly defined user or operational problem.
* Availability of an existing classical baseline.
* Evidence of implemented functionality.
* Data and interface readiness.
* Measurable acceptance criteria.
* Security, privacy and safety requirements.
* Demonstration feasibility and delivery cost.
* Potential buyer interest, which must be independently validated.
* Scope for subsequent commercial deployment.

Do not select a pilot solely because its subject area appears in a government policy.

## 12. Local-value and commercialization evidence

Build local-value evidence progressively and accurately.

Potential evidence includes:

* Australian-based project activities and service delivery.
* Local supplier and partner participation.
* Australian employment, skills development and training.
* Research, engineering or manufacturing activities actually performed in Australia.
* Documented supply-chain contributions.
* Applicable Australian Made claims and their substantiation, where relevant.

Distinguish Australian ownership, Australian operations, Australian research, local assembly and Australian manufacturing. These are different claims and must not be used interchangeably.

## 13. Procurement and proposal gate

Before developing a bid or unsolicited proposal, verify:

1. The opportunity is current and comes from a traceable source.
2. The buyer has a defined need or the proposed engagement has a credible discovery path.
3. Supplier and geographic eligibility have been checked.
4. Required capabilities and delivery capacity are supported by evidence.
5. The procurement route and submission conditions are understood.
6. Material gaps, risks, costs and dependencies are documented.
7. IP ownership, disclosure boundaries and commercialization terms have been reviewed.
8. The proposal can state measurable deliverables and acceptance criteria.

An unsolicited proposal requires its own assessment against the relevant jurisdiction's policy and submission rules; it is not a substitute for responding to an advertised procurement process.

## 14. Review and maintenance

Review this document when:

* A relevant policy or procurement direction changes.
* A new government or private-sector opportunity is identified.
* A QAI/FAEP capability changes readiness status.
* New implementation, test, demonstration or validation evidence becomes available.
* A pilot or project produces material results.
* A legal, security, data or IP dependency changes.

Maintain a change log containing the date, author, sections changed and reason for the change.
