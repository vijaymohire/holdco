# Procurement Alignment Framework

**Status:** Designed / Documented — implementation and supplier readiness to be verified
**Scope:** Australian Commonwealth, NSW, and ACT government procurement, with potential extension to other jurisdictions and private-sector buyers.
**Last reviewed:** 3 October 2026

## 1. Purpose

Define a repeatable method for assessing procurement opportunities and aligning QAI / FAEP products, services, modernization capabilities, research, and pilot demonstrations with buyer requirements.

The framework connects:

* Buyer needs and procurement objectives.
* Supplier eligibility and registration requirements.
* Technical, operational, and commercial capability.
* Evidence of implementation, testing, assurance, and performance.
* Contractual, jurisdictional, security, privacy, and data requirements.
* Bid decisions, delivery commitments, and post-award obligations.

This framework supports opportunity assessment and preparation. It does not establish supplier eligibility, government endorsement, contract award, or compliance by itself.

## 2. Governing Principles

1. **Buyer requirements first:** Use the actual tender, request for quotation, panel, scheme, or other procurement documents as the primary source of opportunity-specific requirements.
2. **Value and outcomes:** Explain the intended public or business benefit, measurable outcomes, costs, risks, and alternatives.
3. **Evidence before claims:** Distinguish documented design from implemented, demonstrated, and independently validated capability.
4. **Applicable rules only:** Determine the buyer, jurisdiction, procurement method, contract scope, and applicable requirements before assessing compliance.
5. **Proportionate response:** Match proposal effort to opportunity value, risk, strategic relevance, and evidence readiness.
6. **Transparent supplier status:** Disclose relevant subcontractors, dependencies, limitations, conflicts, and delivery assumptions.
7. **Protect intellectual property:** Share only the information necessary for the opportunity and identify background IP, third-party IP, foreground IP, licensing, and confidentiality requirements.
8. **No implied endorsement:** Policy alignment or inclusion in a supplier system does not imply government approval, demand, funding eligibility, or a procurement outcome.

## 4. Procurement Opportunity Lifecycle

Use the following lifecycle for each opportunity.

1. **Discover:** Record the notice, buyer, source portal, dates, and opportunity reference.
2. **Classify:** Identify jurisdiction, buyer type, procurement category, opportunity type, estimated value if disclosed, and delivery model.
3. **Qualify:** Check eligibility, supplier registration, scheme or panel access, mandatory conditions, deadlines, and required declarations.
4. **Understand:** Extract the buyer's needs, scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria, service levels, evaluation method, and contract terms.
5. **Map:** Link each requirement to a QAI / FAEP capability, named delivery owner, evidence item, and identified gap.
6. **Assess:** Review technical feasibility, implementation readiness, security, privacy, data handling, IP, subcontracting, commercial viability, and delivery risk.
7. **Decide:** Record a go, conditional-go, partner-first, clarification-required, defer, or no-bid decision with reasons.
8. **Prepare:** Develop the response using the requested format, evidence, pricing, schedule, risk treatment, and contract assumptions.
9. **Review:** Conduct technical, commercial, compliance, and IP reviews before authorised submission.
10. **Submit and track:** Record submission confirmation, clarifications, addenda, interviews, outcome, and feedback.
11. **Deliver and learn:** If awarded, establish contract governance, acceptance evidence, change control, reporting, and lessons learned.

Unsolicited proposals must be handled under the relevant jurisdiction's specific process. They must not be treated as an alternative route around open tender or other mandatory procurement requirements.

## 5. Opportunity Classification

Assign each opportunity one primary classification and any applicable secondary tags.

| Classification                      | Description                                                                      | Initial action                                                                         |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Information / market sounding       | Research, supplier engagement, industry consultation, or request for information | Record buyer needs; avoid treating it as a contract offer.                             |
| Open tender / request for quotation | Competitive request for goods or services                                        | Check mandatory conditions, evaluation criteria, deadlines, and response format.       |
| Panel / scheme / marketplace        | Opportunity requiring access to an established purchasing arrangement            | Verify entry conditions, category scope, and eligibility to participate.               |
| Grant / research collaboration      | Funding or research partnership rather than ordinary purchasing                  | Assess funding eligibility, co-funding, IP terms, milestones, and reporting.           |
| Pilot / proof of concept            | Limited demonstration or trial                                                   | Define baseline, data access, scope, acceptance criteria, safety, and exit conditions. |
| Strategic partnership / subcontract | Delivery with an established supplier or industry partner                        | Define workshare, liability, IP, confidentiality, and customer responsibilities.       |
| Unsolicited proposal                | Supplier-initiated proposal under an applicable formal pathway                   | Verify that the buyer accepts this pathway and follow its published rules.             |
| Private-sector opportunity          | Commercial project outside government procurement                                | Apply the buyer's commercial, technical, security, and contracting requirements.       |

Classification is an initial routing decision, not a judgement that an opportunity is suitable or eligible.

## 6. Requirement-to-Evidence Mapping

Maintain a requirement register for every opportunity.

| Field                 | Required information                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Opportunity ID        | Unique internal reference                                                                                                                       |
| Requirement ID        | Stable identifier for each mandatory or scored requirement                                                                                      |
| Requirement text      | Accurate summary or controlled quotation from the source                                                                                        |
| Source                | Tender section, specification, policy, contract clause, or portal URL                                                                           |
| Requirement type      | Mandatory, scored, contractual, assurance, commercial, or informational                                                                         |
| Applicability         | Applicable, not applicable with reason, or clarification required                                                                               |
| QAI / FAEP capability | Product, service, framework, module, or delivery process                                                                                        |
| Capability status     | Planned, designed/documented, partially implemented, implemented—verification pending, demonstrated, validated, not assessed, or not applicable |
| Evidence reference    | Repository path, test record, demonstration, certificate, case study, or other controlled evidence                                              |
| Gap and risk          | Missing capability, unverified claim, dependency, or delivery exposure                                                                          |
| Action owner          | Person or organisation responsible                                                                                                              |
| Due date              | Date required to close the gap                                                                                                                  |
| Review outcome        | Open, accepted, closed, or exception approved by an authorised reviewer                                                                         |

Do not mark a requirement compliant solely because a relevant document exists. Confirm that the evidence actually supports the requirement and applies to the proposed delivery scope.

## 7. QAI / FAEP Capability Alignment

Potential capability groupings include:

* **Products and platforms:** QAI Hub, QAI OS, QAI Datacenter System, QAI Processor, QAI Runtime, and related platform components.
* **AI and agentic capabilities:** QAI Agent Framework, QAI LLM DevOps Framework, and relevant orchestration or lifecycle tooling.
* **Quantum and hybrid research:** QAI Research Hub, QAI PoC Lab, quantum algorithms, and hybrid quantum-classical experimentation.
* **Engineering and deployment:** QAI ProductDev Toolkit, QAI ProductDeploy Toolkit, QAI BareMetal Framework, and integration services.
* **Industry solutions:** Digital Farm CPS / Digital Twin and other validated domain-specific demonstrators.
* **Professional services:** Assessment, architecture, modernization, systems integration, training, research, and implementation services where actually available.

This is a portfolio mapping, not a claim that every component is production-ready or commercially available. Each proposed capability must be checked against the current implementation and evidence register.

## 8. Supplier Readiness Checklist

Before committing to a response, verify:

* [ ] Correct legal supplier identity and authorised signatory.
* [ ] Applicable business identifiers and supplier registration completed where required.
* [ ] Relevant panel, scheme, marketplace, or prequalification status confirmed.
* [ ] Mandatory eligibility and participation conditions checked.
* [ ] Required insurance, financial, professional, and reference evidence identified.
* [ ] Technical requirements and acceptance criteria mapped.
* [ ] Security, privacy, data residency, hosting, and access requirements assessed.
* [ ] AI, automation, quantum, and other technology claims are supported by appropriate evidence.
* [ ] Delivery capacity, dependencies, subcontractors, and schedule assessed.
* [ ] Pricing, whole-of-life costs, assumptions, and commercial terms reviewed.
* [ ] Background IP, third-party IP, proposed foreground IP, licensing, and confidentiality reviewed.
* [ ] Mandatory declarations, conflict-of-interest checks, and supplier conduct requirements addressed.
* [ ] Approval to submit obtained and submission method tested.

A checklist item may be marked not applicable only with a recorded reason. The applicable procurement documents determine the final requirements.

## 9. Go / No-Go Decision

Use a documented decision record rather than an unsupported numerical score.

**Proceed** when mandatory eligibility is satisfied, the scope is understood, evidence supports the material claims, delivery is feasible, and commercial and IP risks are acceptable.

**Proceed conditionally** when specific gaps can be closed before submission or contract execution and an authorised owner accepts the action plan.

**Seek clarification** when a material requirement, buyer expectation, or contractual condition is ambiguous.

**Partner first** when delivery requires a qualified supplier, local delivery capacity, a specialist certification, or an established panel relationship that is not currently available.

**Defer or no-bid** when mandatory eligibility is unmet, material claims cannot be evidenced, deadlines cannot be met, or risks cannot be acceptably controlled.

Record the rationale, approver, unresolved conditions, and review date.

## 10. Australian Local Value and Supplier Claims

Build local-value evidence progressively and accurately. Relevant evidence may include:

* Legal supplier structure and place of establishment.
* Actual Australian employees, contractors, facilities, and operational capacity.
* Australian research, engineering, testing, manufacturing, or service activities.
* Australian subcontractors and partner contributions.
* Local supply-chain expenditure and support arrangements.
* Origin and provenance evidence for products, components, and services.

Do not claim Australian ownership, Australian manufacture, local employment, local content, or SME status without evidence that satisfies the applicable definition and opportunity requirements. An Australian business presence alone does not establish that a product is Australian Made.

## 11. IP and Commercialization Controls

Before sharing detailed QAI / FAEP material:

1. Identify background IP and its owner.
2. Separate public descriptions from confidential implementation details, source code, unpublished inventions, and research records.
3. Confirm the permitted disclosure purpose and recipient.
4. Review tender confidentiality, disclosure, records-management, and IP clauses.
5. Identify proposed foreground IP, ownership, licensing, reuse rights, and third-party restrictions.
6. Obtain appropriate review before accepting terms that could transfer or restrict core portfolio IP.
7. Record what was disclosed, under which authority, and when.

Use proportionate disclosure and obtain qualified legal advice where IP ownership or contract terms are material. Do not assume that a non-disclosure agreement overrides all tender or statutory disclosure obligations.

## 12. Email-to-Opportunity Intake

Incoming NSW, ACT, Commonwealth, and partner emails should be processed as follows:

1. Record sender, date, subject, source portal, buyer, and opportunity reference.
2. Preserve the original notice and attachments in the controlled project record.
3. Extract deadlines, mandatory conditions, scope, evaluation criteria, and contact details.
4. Classify the opportunity using Section 5.
5. Create or update the requirement-to-evidence register.
6. Identify questions, gaps, dependencies, and decision deadlines.
7. Assign an owner and next action.
8. Record the final decision and link it to the source email and opportunity record.

Emails are intake signals, not authoritative substitutes for current tender documents, formal addenda, or buyer instructions.

## 13. Initial Pilot and Demonstration Strategy

The Digital Farm CPS / Digital Twin is a candidate initial demonstrator because it provides a defined domain for documenting assets, workflows, interfaces, operating scenarios, and measurable outcomes.

Before using it in a procurement response, confirm:

* The demonstrated functions and current implementation status.
* The classical baseline and the specific problem addressed.
* Data sources, assumptions, and limitations.
* Test scenarios, evaluation measures, and recorded results.
* Security, safety, privacy, and operational constraints.
* Acceptance criteria and what remains experimental.
* Whether the buyer's problem and procurement scope genuinely match the demonstrator.

Do not claim quantum advantage, production readiness, or quantified benefits without appropriate comparative evidence.

## 14. Governance and Continuous Improvement

Maintain the following controlled records:

* Procurement source register.
* Opportunity register and classification.
* Requirement-to-evidence matrix.
* Supplier-readiness checklist.
* Capability gaps and corrective-action register.
* Go / no-go decision log.
* Proposal and submission register.
* IP disclosure and contract-review record.
* Pilot results and lessons-learned record.

Review this framework when relevant procurement rules change, a new jurisdiction is added, supplier status changes, or a material opportunity exposes a process gap.
