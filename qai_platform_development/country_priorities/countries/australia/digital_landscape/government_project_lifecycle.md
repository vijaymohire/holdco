# Australian Government Project Lifecycle

**Status:** Designed / Documented — project-specific applicability and implementation to be verified
**Owner:** Bhadale IT — QAI / FAEP
**Scope:** Commonwealth, NSW, ACT, and other Australian government project environments, subject to jurisdiction-specific assessment
**Last reviewed:** 3 October 2026

## 1. Purpose

Define a reference lifecycle for investigating, planning, procuring, delivering, assuring, accepting, and operating technology-enabled projects in Australian government environments.

The lifecycle provides a common analysis structure for mapping government needs and project requirements to potential QAI / FAEP capabilities, delivery activities, evidence, risks, and commercial opportunities.

**Important:** This is a proposed reference model for Bhadale IT's planning and project analysis. It is not an official Australian Government lifecycle and does not replace applicable legislation, procurement rules, agency procedures, funding conditions, assurance frameworks, or contractual obligations.

## 2. Objectives

The lifecycle aims to:

* Connect public needs and agency objectives to measurable project outcomes.
* Identify the business problem before selecting a technology solution.
* Support evidence-based business cases, options analysis, and investment decisions.
* Identify procurement, partnership, and delivery pathways.
* Integrate architecture, security, privacy, data governance, accessibility, and assurance considerations.
* Establish clear responsibilities, decision gates, and acceptance criteria.
* Map potential QAI / FAEP capabilities to defined needs without assuming product suitability or readiness.
* Measure outcomes and benefits after deployment.

## 3. Reference Lifecycle

The following stages form a common planning model. Projects may combine, repeat, reorder, or omit activities where permitted by their governing arrangements.

| Stage | Name                                             | Principal output                                         |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| G0    | Public need and strategic alignment              | Problem statement and strategic fit                      |
| G1    | Discovery and feasibility                        | Requirements, baseline, and initial options              |
| G2    | Business case and investment planning            | Cost, benefit, risk, and options assessment              |
| G3    | Governance, architecture, and assurance planning | Governance model, architecture, and assurance plan       |
| G4    | Procurement and sourcing                         | Approved sourcing approach and procurement records       |
| G5    | Solution design and delivery planning            | Baseline design, delivery plan, and acceptance criteria  |
| G6    | Implementation and integration                   | Implemented scope and integration evidence               |
| G7    | Verification, validation, and assurance          | Test results, risk decisions, and readiness evidence     |
| G8    | Acceptance and transition to service             | Acceptance decision and operational handover             |
| G9    | Operations and service management                | Service performance, support, and incident records       |
| G10   | Benefits realisation and evaluation              | Benefits evidence, evaluation, and improvement decisions |
| G11   | Closure, renewal, or scale-up                    | Closure records or approved next-stage decision          |

These stage identifiers are internal reference labels. They must not be confused with formal Commonwealth Gateway review numbers or NSW Gateway gates.

## 4. Stage Definitions

### G0. Public Need and Strategic Alignment

**Purpose:** Establish the public-service, agency, or operational problem.

Activities:

* Identify the affected community, service users, agency, or operational stakeholders.
* Document the problem, current constraints, and consequences of inaction.
* Identify relevant government priorities and agency objectives.
* Define intended outcomes and preliminary measures of success.
* Identify the accountable sponsor and decision authority.

Outputs:

* Problem statement.
* Strategic-alignment record.
* Initial stakeholder map.
* Preliminary outcome measures.

**Decision:** Is there a sufficiently defined need to justify further investigation?

### G1. Discovery and Feasibility

**Purpose:** Determine the scope of the problem and the available options.

Activities:

* Gather stakeholder and operational requirements.
* Document the current process, systems, assets, data, and dependencies.
* Establish a baseline using available evidence.
* Assess existing government or industry solutions and opportunities for reuse.
* Identify technical, organisational, legal, security, privacy, and delivery constraints.
* Assess whether a pilot or proof of concept would resolve material uncertainties.

Outputs:

* Requirements catalogue.
* Current-state assessment.
* Options register.
* Initial risk and dependency register.
* Feasibility findings.

**Decision:** Is there a plausible, evidence-supported path to a useful outcome?

### G2. Business Case and Investment Planning

**Purpose:** Support an informed decision about whether and how to invest.

Activities:

* Compare feasible options, including non-technology alternatives where relevant.
* Estimate whole-of-life costs and resource requirements.
* Assess expected benefits, risks, dependencies, and uncertainty.
* Examine market capacity, existing solutions, and potential suppliers.
* Define measurable benefits, target dates, and accountable owners.
* Document funding assumptions and affordability constraints.

Outputs:

* Business case or proportionate investment proposal.
* Options and cost-benefit assessment.
* Benefits-realisation plan.
* Funding and resource assumptions.
* Decision record.

**Decision:** Is the proposal sufficiently justified and authorised to progress?

The required business-case format and approval process depend on the applicable jurisdiction, agency, investment framework, project risk, and funding arrangements.

### G3. Governance, Architecture, and Assurance Planning

**Purpose:** Establish how the project will be governed, designed, controlled, and assessed.

Activities:

* Define the sponsor, senior responsible owner where applicable, project manager, technical authorities, and assurance responsibilities.
* Establish scope, governance, reporting, risk, issue, and change-control arrangements.
* Map applicable legal, policy, contractual, security, privacy, accessibility, and data requirements.
* Develop logical, application, integration, and deployment architecture views.
* Define information ownership, interface boundaries, and data flows.
* Determine required assurance reviews, approvals, and evidence.
* Establish measurable acceptance criteria.

Outputs:

* Governance and responsibility model.
* Architecture and design decisions.
* Requirements traceability matrix.
* Risk and assurance plan.
* Acceptance and verification strategy.

**Decision:** Are the project controls, responsibilities, and delivery approach sufficiently defined to proceed?

### G4. Procurement and Sourcing

**Purpose:** Select an appropriate and authorised method of obtaining the required products, services, or delivery capability.

Potential pathways include:

* Existing government arrangements or panels.
* Open or selective approaches to market, where permitted.
* Approved competitive procurement processes.
* Research and development collaboration.
* A partner-led pilot or subcontracting arrangement.
* Other lawful sourcing or commercial models permitted by the relevant authority.

Activities:

* Confirm the applicable procurement framework and approvals.
* Define requirements and evaluation criteria.
* Assess market options and value for money.
* Identify intellectual property, licensing, data, confidentiality, and security requirements.
* Establish supplier due diligence and conflict-of-interest controls.
* Document evaluation, approvals, and contract decisions.

Outputs:

* Procurement strategy.
* Requirements and evaluation documents.
* Sourcing and approval records.
* Contract or collaboration terms.
* Supplier and delivery-risk assessment.

**Decision:** Is the sourcing approach authorised, adequately documented, and appropriate to the requirement?

A capability mapping or unsolicited proposal does not guarantee a procurement opportunity, eligibility, contract award, or government endorsement.

### G5. Solution Design and Delivery Planning

**Purpose:** Translate approved requirements into an implementable solution and delivery plan.

Activities:

* Baseline the scope, requirements, architecture, and dependencies.
* Define milestones, resources, budget, and delivery responsibilities.
* Design interfaces, data handling, access controls, and operational processes.
* Establish development, configuration, testing, and deployment methods.
* Define fallback behaviour, recovery arrangements, and exception handling.
* Establish delivery metrics and acceptance criteria.

Outputs:

* Solution design.
* Delivery plan and milestone baseline.
* Interface and data specifications.
* Test and deployment plans.
* Updated risk and dependency registers.

### G6. Implementation and Integration

**Purpose:** Build, configure, acquire, or integrate the approved solution.

Activities:

* Implement or configure the agreed components.
* Integrate approved data sources, applications, and external systems.
* Maintain version, configuration, and change records.
* Perform development and integration testing.
* Record deviations from the approved design.
* Update implementation status and unresolved risks.

Outputs:

* Implemented project scope.
* Integration records.
* Configuration and version records.
* Test results.
* Updated architecture and requirements traceability.

Implementation status must be supported by evidence for the stated scope.

### G7. Verification, Validation, and Assurance

**Purpose:** Establish whether the solution meets defined requirements and is suitable for its intended use.

Activities:

* Verify functional and non-functional requirements.
* Test security, privacy, accessibility, interoperability, performance, and resilience as applicable.
* Validate the solution with representative users and operating scenarios.
* Review risks, dependencies, defects, and outstanding exceptions.
* Obtain required technical, operational, security, and independent assurance reviews.
* Confirm whether acceptance criteria have been met.

Outputs:

* Verification and validation reports.
* Defect and remediation records.
* Risk and exception decisions.
* Assurance findings.
* Readiness recommendation.

**Decision:** Is there sufficient evidence to proceed to acceptance, or is further remediation required?

A successful demonstration does not by itself establish production readiness, regulatory compliance, or independent assurance.

### G8. Acceptance and Transition to Service

**Purpose:** Obtain the required acceptance decision and prepare the solution for operation.

Activities:

* Confirm contractual and operational acceptance criteria.
* Complete required approvals and resolve material defects.
* Prepare support procedures, user documentation, training, and escalation paths.
* Establish service ownership, monitoring, backup, recovery, and incident processes.
* Confirm handover of agreed records, credentials, licences, and configuration information through approved secure channels.

Outputs:

* Acceptance decision.
* Operational handover pack.
* Support and escalation model.
* Service-level and performance measures.
* Outstanding-action register.

Acceptance authority and transition requirements are determined by the relevant agency, contract, and governance arrangements.

### G9. Operations and Service Management

**Purpose:** Operate the accepted service and maintain its agreed level of performance and control.

Activities:

* Monitor availability, performance, service quality, and cost.
* Manage incidents, problems, changes, vulnerabilities, and releases.
* Maintain approved configurations, access controls, and operational records.
* Test backup, restoration, and recovery procedures as required.
* Review supplier performance and service dependencies.
* Record issues that may affect outcomes, security, or continuity.

Outputs:

* Service and operational reports.
* Incident and change records.
* Performance and cost measures.
* Recovery-test evidence.
* Improvement backlog.

### G10. Benefits Realisation and Evaluation

**Purpose:** Determine whether the project achieved its intended outcomes.

Activities:

* Compare actual results with the original baseline and approved targets.
* Assess realised benefits, whole-of-life costs, and unintended effects.
* Gather stakeholder and service-user feedback.
* Identify unresolved issues and opportunities for improvement.
* Record lessons and update the benefits-realisation plan.

Outputs:

* Benefits and evaluation report.
* Outcome metrics.
* Lessons-learned register.
* Remediation and improvement recommendations.

### G11. Closure, Renewal, or Scale-up

**Purpose:** Decide whether to close the project, continue the service, renew the arrangement, or expand its scope.

Activities:

* Confirm completion of closure and handover obligations.
* Retain required records and manage data and asset disposition.
* Review contract end dates, licensing, dependencies, and support arrangements.
* Assess whether further investment is justified by the evidence.
* Document the next decision and its approval requirements.

Outputs:

* Closure or transition report.
* Records and asset disposition evidence.
* Renewal or scale-up business case where required.
* Approved next-stage decision.

Scale-up should be based on demonstrated outcomes, operational capability, funding, risk, and applicable approvals.

## 5. Jurisdiction-Specific Adaptation

Use the lifecycle as a common reference while maintaining separate records for jurisdiction-specific obligations.

| Environment                    | Initial source and assessment focus                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Australian Government          | Commonwealth investment, digital investment oversight, assurance, and procurement requirements.                                           |
| NSW Government                 | NSW Investment Framework, Business Case Guidelines, Gateway Policy, Digital Assurance Framework, and applicable procurement requirements. |
| ACT Government                 | ACT technology directions, procurement arrangements, agency-specific approvals, and applicable security and privacy requirements.         |
| Other Australian jurisdictions | Identify the relevant jurisdiction's investment, procurement, digital, and assurance frameworks before applying the model.                |

The number, names, sequence, thresholds, and mandatory nature of formal review gates vary by framework and project. Confirm the current requirements with the responsible authority.

## 7. Initial Application: Digital Farm Pilot

Use the Digital Farm CPS / Digital Twin as a bounded candidate pilot for testing the lifecycle.

Initial activities:

1. Define the agricultural or operational problem and target users.
2. Inventory existing assets, data sources, interfaces, and workflows.
3. Document the classical baseline and current operating process.
4. Define measurable pilot outcomes and acceptance criteria.
5. Assess relevant data, privacy, security, safety, and contractual requirements.
6. Identify whether simulation, emulation, or physical execution is appropriate for each experiment.
7. Implement only the agreed pilot scope.
8. Test the solution and record results against the baseline.
9. Review costs, limitations, risks, and operational implications.
10. Decide whether to stop, revise, extend, or propose a subsequent phase.

The pilot does not establish general agricultural market demand, government procurement eligibility, or quantum advantage without separate supporting evidence.

## 8. Lifecycle Evidence Register

For each material stage or decision, record:

* Project ID and lifecycle stage.
* Requirement or decision ID.
* Applicable jurisdiction and source.
* Responsible owner and approving authority.
* Required deliverable or acceptance criterion.
* Evidence reference and evidence date.
* Current status and unresolved gaps.
* Risks, dependencies, and exceptions.
* Next action, owner, and due date.
* Decision outcome and review trigger.

Evidence may include approved business cases, architecture records, procurement documents, contracts, test reports, assurance findings, acceptance records, operational reports, and benefits evaluations.

Protect confidential government information, personal information, third-party material, and Bhadale IT intellectual property according to the applicable obligations and agreements.
