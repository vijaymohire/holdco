**Status:** Planned

**Scope:** Australian government and private-sector digital project environments

## 1. Purpose

Document the Australian digital project landscape relevant to QAI (Quantum AI) and FAEP (Federated Autonomous Ecosystem Platform), including government and jurisdictional technology directions, digital architecture, project lifecycles, procurement requirements, assurance expectations, and commercialization pathways.

This folder provides a structured reference for identifying relevant requirements, understanding project delivery environments, and mapping potential QAI / FAEP capabilities to documented needs.

Policy alignment or architectural correspondence does not, by itself, establish compliance, product readiness, customer demand, procurement eligibility, or government endorsement.

## 2. Scope

The initial scope covers:

* **Commonwealth:** Relevant Australian Government digital, technology, cyber security, procurement, and innovation directions.
* **State and territory:** Requirements and guidance relevant to the jurisdictions being assessed, initially including New South Wales (NSW) and the Australian Capital Territory (ACT).
* **Government projects:** Planning, procurement, delivery, assurance, acceptance, and operational transition.
* **Private-sector projects:** Discovery, business cases, architecture, delivery, validation, deployment, and commercial operation.
* **Digital architecture:** Enterprise, solution, application, data, integration, infrastructure, cloud, edge, AI, and cyber-physical systems.
* **QAI / FAEP alignment:** Potential capability mappings, dependencies, implementation status, evidence, and gaps.

Applicability must be assessed for each project, customer, jurisdiction, contract, and deployment context.

## 4. Research and Mapping Workflow

Use the following workflow when developing each document:

1. **Identify the source** — locate an authoritative government, regulator, standards body, procurement authority, or other relevant primary source.
2. **Verify the source** — record its title, issuing organisation, canonical URL, publication or revision date where available, and date accessed.
3. **Extract requirements** — capture the relevant provision, scope, intended audience, applicability conditions, and any stated exceptions.
4. **Classify the requirement** — distinguish legislation, regulation, mandatory policy, contractual obligation, technical standard, guidance, and strategic priority.
5. **Map potential capabilities** — identify relevant QAI / FAEP products, services, architecture components, or development work.
6. **Assess the gap** — record what is documented, implemented, tested, demonstrated, independently assessed, or still unknown.
7. **Define follow-up actions** — assign an owner, required evidence, target date, and closure criteria where appropriate.
8. **Review changes** — update mappings when authoritative sources, contracts, architectures, or implementation evidence change.

## 6. Evidence and Status Management

Use explicit statuses when documenting requirements and capabilities:

* **Not Assessed** — applicability or implementation has not been evaluated.
* **Planned** — work is proposed but not yet completed.
* **Designed / Documented** — a design or supporting documentation exists.
* **Partially Implemented** — only part of the intended capability is implemented.
* **Implemented — Verification Pending** — implementation is reported, but defined verification remains incomplete.
* **Demonstrated** — operation has been shown in a defined demonstration environment.
* **Validated** — defined acceptance or validation criteria have been met, with evidence recorded.
* **Not Applicable — Justified** — non-applicability has been assessed and documented.

For each material mapping, record the requirement ID, source, mapped capability, status, evidence reference, gap, action owner, and review date.

Do not mark a requirement compliant solely because a related framework, architecture document, or product description exists.
