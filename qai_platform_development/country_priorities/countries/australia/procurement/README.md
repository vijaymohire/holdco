# Australian Procurement Framework — QAI / FAEP

**Status:** In Progress
**Purpose:** Establish a structured reference for identifying Australian public-sector procurement requirements, supplier pathways, technology assurance obligations, and commercialization opportunities relevant to Bhadale IT's QAI / FAEP portfolio.
**Last reviewed:** 3 October 2026
**Maintainer:** Bhadale IT / QAI-FAEP
**Scope:** Commonwealth, state and territory, local-government, and relevant public-sector procurement pathways.

---

## 1. Objectives

This directory provides a practical starting point for understanding how QAI / FAEP products, services, modernization capabilities, and research offerings may be prepared for government and public-sector opportunities.

The objectives are to:

1. Identify authoritative procurement policies, rules, portals, and supplier guidance.
2. Distinguish requirements applying to buyers from requirements applying to suppliers.
3. Identify registrations, qualifications, certifications, insurance, and other participation conditions that may apply to specific opportunities.
4. Map procurement requirements to relevant QAI / FAEP capabilities and supporting evidence.
5. Identify gaps in supplier readiness, product maturity, assurance, and commercial delivery.
6. Prepare proportionate responses for tenders, requests for quotation, expressions of interest, panels, demonstrations, and pilot opportunities.
7. Protect intellectual property and confidential information during engagement and contracting.
8. Establish a repeatable process for monitoring opportunities and assessing whether to participate.

**Guiding principle:** A procurement opportunity should be pursued only after its actual requirements, eligibility conditions, delivery obligations, and commercial implications have been assessed.

## 2. Procurement scope and applicability

Procurement rules differ by jurisdiction, purchasing entity, procurement category, estimated contract value, funding source, and procurement method.

| Scope                                | Initial reference                             | Purpose                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Australian Government                | Commonwealth Procurement Rules (CPRs)         | Understand Commonwealth procurement principles and applicable requirements.                                      |
| Commonwealth opportunities           | AusTender                                     | Find published business opportunities, annual procurement plans, and awarded-contract information.               |
| NSW Government                       | NSW Procurement Policy Framework              | Assess NSW government procurement requirements and processes.                                                    |
| NSW opportunities                    | NSW procurement and tender channels           | Identify relevant opportunities and supplier instructions.                                                       |
| ACT Government                       | Procurement ACT                               | Review supplier registration, procurement policies, and current and future opportunities.                        |
| Other states and territories         | Relevant jurisdictional procurement authority | Establish local requirements before pursuing an opportunity.                                                     |
| Local government                     | Individual council or procurement arrangement | Check local tender portals, supplier conditions, and purchasing rules.                                           |
| Research and innovation              | Relevant agency, institution, or program      | Determine whether the mechanism is a procurement, grant, research agreement, investment, or another arrangement. |
| Private-sector and industry partners | Customer or partner requirements              | Assess commercial terms, technical assurance, delivery, and contractual obligations.                             |

The Commonwealth Procurement Rules provide a framework for Commonwealth entities to pursue value for money, competition, ethical conduct, transparency, accountability, and appropriate procurement risk management. Their application depends on the entity and procurement circumstances. [Official CPR reference](https://www.finance.gov.au/government/procurement/commonwealth-procurement-rules).

### 3.4 Other jurisdictions and local government

Before expanding into another state, territory, or council, establish a jurisdiction-specific source register containing:

* Procurement authority and current policy.
* Official tender and opportunity portal.
* Supplier registration and qualification process.
* Applicable digital, cybersecurity, privacy, data, and AI policies.
* Contracting terms, insurance, and liability requirements.
* Local participation or industry policies that actually apply.
* Relevant procurement panels, marketplaces, or standing offers.
* Applicable legislation and current threshold rules.

Do not assume that Commonwealth, NSW, ACT, or one council's procurement arrangements apply to another buyer.

## 4. Requirement classification

Record the nature of every requirement before assigning a gap or planning a response.

| Classification             | Meaning                                                                         | Required treatment                                               |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mandatory                  | Requirement imposed by applicable law, policy, or tender conditions             | Verify applicability and evidence compliance.                    |
| Contractual                | Obligation arising from a proposed or executed agreement                        | Review scope, cost, liability, and delivery implications.        |
| Participation condition    | Qualification or condition for a supplier to participate                        | Verify eligibility before investing in a bid.                    |
| Evaluation criterion       | Published factor used to evaluate submissions                                   | Map to a specific response and evidence.                         |
| Recommended practice       | Guidance that may improve delivery or assurance                                 | Assess proportional adoption.                                    |
| Opportunity signal         | Policy or strategy suggesting a possible area of interest                       | Validate a real buyer need before treating it as an opportunity. |
| Voluntary/adopted standard | Standard or control adopted by the supplier or required by a specific agreement | Record its actual status and scope.                              |
| Unverified                 | Requirement, interpretation, or applicability not yet confirmed                 | Assign an owner and verification action.                         |

Do not describe a recommendation, strategic policy statement, or internal QAI design principle as a mandatory procurement requirement unless an authoritative source establishes that status.

## 5. Supplier readiness

Maintain a supplier-readiness assessment independently from product maturity.

### 5.1 Business and commercial readiness

Assess as applicable:

* Legal business identity and contracting entity.
* Australian business presence or other eligibility conditions.
* Tax, invoicing, and financial administration.
* Insurance and professional indemnity requirements.
* Relevant experience and verifiable delivery references.
* Subcontractors, delivery partners, and responsibility boundaries.
* Financial capacity and delivery continuity.
* Conflict-of-interest declarations and ethical conduct.
* Applicable supplier registrations, prequalification, and panel arrangements.
* Ability to meet the proposed delivery schedule and service levels.

Where Bhadale IT proposes to contract from outside Australia, determine whether the specific opportunity permits that arrangement and whether local incorporation, an Australian delivery partner, local staffing, or other conditions apply. Do not assume that a particular structure is universally required or sufficient.

### 5.2 Technical and operational readiness

Assess as applicable:

* Defined product or service scope.
* Architecture, interfaces, and deployment model.
* Supported hardware, cloud, edge, and on-premises environments.
* Identity and access management.
* Cybersecurity controls and vulnerability management.
* Privacy, data residency, and data-handling arrangements.
* Logging, monitoring, backup, recovery, and support.
* Integration and interoperability.
* Testing, acceptance criteria, and performance evidence.
* Accessibility, safety, resilience, and operational continuity.
* Release management, maintenance, and incident handling.

### 5.3 Evidence readiness

Prepare evidence that is relevant to the actual procurement, such as:

* Capability statements and product descriptions.
* Demonstration scripts and screenshots.
* Architecture and data-flow diagrams.
* Test results, benchmark methods, and known limitations.
* Security and privacy documentation.
* Delivery plans, risk registers, and acceptance criteria.
* References, case studies, and partner credentials where available.
* Pricing assumptions, service boundaries, and support models.
* Intellectual-property and licensing schedules.

A document describing an intended capability is not, by itself, proof that the capability has been implemented, tested, or accepted.

## 7. Opportunity assessment workflow

Use the following sequence before committing significant time or money to an opportunity.

1. **Discover** — Find the opportunity through an official portal, buyer notice, partner, or other traceable source.
2. **Classify** — Identify the buyer, jurisdiction, procurement mechanism, scope, deadlines, and funding or contracting arrangement.
3. **Verify** — Check mandatory conditions, supplier eligibility, submission requirements, and current rules.
4. **Qualify** — Assess strategic fit, delivery capability, commercial viability, resource requirements, and conflicts.
5. **Map** — Connect the buyer's stated requirements to specific QAI / FAEP capabilities.
6. **Evidence** — Identify what is demonstrated, what remains unverified, and what must be produced before submission.
7. **Decide** — Record a go/no-go decision and its rationale.
8. **Respond** — Prepare a compliant, proportionate submission with traceable evidence.
9. **Deliver** — If awarded, establish contract governance, acceptance criteria, reporting, and change control.
10. **Learn** — Capture results, feedback, reusable evidence, and improvement actions.

**Recommended operating sequence:** Reuse → Connect → Augment → Automate → Optimize → Innovate.

Where possible, demonstrate a bounded, measurable pilot before proposing a larger deployment. A pilot must still meet the buyer's actual procurement, security, safety, and contractual requirements.

## 8. Opportunity maturity

Track the maturity of each opportunity separately from the technical readiness of the proposed solution.

| Stage                 | Definition                                                                                 | Evidence needed to progress                                         |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Policy-aligned        | An official policy or strategy is relevant to the general problem area                     | Cited official source and a documented alignment rationale          |
| Problem hypothesis    | A plausible buyer problem has been identified                                              | Problem statement and assumptions                                   |
| Stakeholder-validated | A relevant stakeholder has confirmed the problem and context                               | Meeting notes, written confirmation, or discovery record            |
| Technically assessed  | Proposed capabilities and dependencies have been evaluated                                 | Architecture review, feasibility assessment, and gap register       |
| Pilot-ready           | Scope, responsibilities, data, acceptance criteria, and resources are sufficiently defined | Pilot plan, risk assessment, delivery estimate, and approvals       |
| Demonstrated          | The agreed demonstration has been executed                                                 | Demonstration record and observed results                           |
| Validated             | Results have been assessed against predefined acceptance criteria                          | Test evidence, stakeholder review, and recorded limitations         |
| Commercially assessed | Delivery and business terms have been evaluated                                            | Cost model, pricing assumptions, support model, and contract review |
| Contracted            | An authorised agreement has been executed                                                  | Executed contract or other valid award documentation                |
| Delivered             | Contractual outputs have been completed and assessed                                       | Acceptance records, operational handover, and lessons learned       |

Policy alignment does not prove customer demand, eligibility, technical suitability, funding availability, or a likelihood of contract award.

## 9. Procurement response and bid controls

Before submitting any tender, request for quotation, or expression of interest, complete a bid-readiness review.

### 9.1 Compliance checklist

* [ ] Correct procurement notice and current documents identified.
* [ ] Buyer, jurisdiction, scope, deadlines, and submission channel verified.
* [ ] Eligibility and participation conditions checked.
* [ ] Required registrations, declarations, insurance, and qualifications reviewed.
* [ ] Mandatory requirements mapped to explicit responses and evidence.
* [ ] Evaluation criteria mapped to relevant differentiators and proof.
* [ ] Technical scope, exclusions, dependencies, and assumptions documented.
* [ ] Delivery plan, resourcing, schedule, and service levels assessed.
* [ ] Pricing, taxes, currency, payment terms, and commercial risks reviewed.
* [ ] Privacy, cybersecurity, data handling, and subcontractor arrangements assessed.
* [ ] IP ownership, licensing, background IP, and newly developed IP addressed.
* [ ] Confidentiality and permitted disclosure conditions checked.
* [ ] Required approvals obtained and final submission independently checked.
* [ ] Submission receipt and final version retained.

A completed checklist is an internal control; it does not replace legal review or the buyer's own evaluation.

## 10. Intellectual property and commercialization safeguards

QAI / FAEP assets may include software, source code, models, architectures, datasets, documentation, research results, designs, and patentable concepts.

For each procurement or proposed pilot:

1. Identify background IP owned or controlled before the engagement.
2. Identify the specific assets proposed for disclosure, licensing, or delivery.
3. Separate background IP from project-specific deliverables and any newly created IP.
4. Define permitted use, access, modification, redistribution, and sublicensing.
5. Document confidentiality, data rights, publication, and research-use conditions.
6. Review ownership and licensing clauses before agreeing to a contract.
7. Limit technical disclosures to what is necessary for evaluation and delivery.
8. Record third-party software, open-source, dataset, and model obligations.
9. Obtain appropriate legal advice where ownership, patentability, or licensing rights are material.

**Portfolio boundary:** Participation in one tender, pilot, or commercial venture should not be assumed to transfer rights to the wider QAI / FAEP portfolio. The contract must explicitly define the rights granted and the assets covered.

Do not include confidential source code, unpublished patent claims, sensitive credentials, or proprietary research details in public tender material unless disclosure is authorised and appropriate.

## 12. Initial implementation plan

### Phase 1 — Establish the baseline

* Maintain the Commonwealth, NSW, and ACT source registers.
* Identify relevant official procurement portals and supplier guides.
* Review the current supplier's legal and commercial readiness.
* Establish a reusable procurement compliance checklist.
* Create the opportunity and evidence registers.

### Phase 2 — Map capabilities and gaps

* Map the initial Digital Farm pilot and selected QAI / FAEP capabilities to clearly defined buyer needs.
* Distinguish documented design from implemented and demonstrated functionality.
* Prepare reusable capability statements, diagrams, demonstration evidence, and technical limitations.
* Identify gaps in security, privacy, operational support, contractual readiness, and delivery capacity.

### Phase 3 — Validate opportunities

* Review actual notices and advance procurement plans.
* Seek stakeholder discovery where appropriate.
* Assess the procurement mechanism and participation conditions.
* Record a reasoned go/no-go decision before preparing a resource-intensive bid.

### Phase 4 — Demonstrate and improve

* Conduct a bounded demonstration or pilot where a suitable pathway exists.
* Agree on success measures and acceptance criteria in advance.
* Record outcomes, limitations, costs, and stakeholder feedback.
* Update the evidence register and commercialization plan.

### Phase 5 — Expand jurisdictional coverage

After the initial Commonwealth, NSW, and ACT baseline is established, add other states, territories, local government, and relevant industry procurement pathways according to validated opportunities and available delivery capacity.
