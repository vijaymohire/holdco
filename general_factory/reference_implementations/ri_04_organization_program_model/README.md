# RI-04 — Organisation and Program Framework

## 1. Purpose

RI-04 defines a reusable reference model for translating strategic intent into an operating model, programs, capabilities, specialist teams, projects and measurable outcomes.

It provides a structured way to align organisational responsibilities, decision authority, specialist expertise, shared capabilities and project delivery with the objectives of HoldCo and its participating organisations.

The model supports a flexible operating structure in which common capabilities can serve multiple programs and projects, while project-specific teams and resources are assembled according to validated needs.

It is intended to help distinguish strategic direction, portfolio decisions, program coordination, capability ownership, working-group responsibilities and project execution.

RI-04 is a technology-neutral reference specification. It does not prescribe a particular legal structure, organisational chart, management methodology or software platform.

## 2. Design Principles

- **Strategy-to-outcome alignment:** Connect strategic intent to defined programs, projects, deliverables and measurable outcomes.
- **Explicit accountability:** Identify accountable owners, decision rights, responsibilities and escalation paths.
- **Separation of concerns:** Distinguish governance, program coordination, capability management and project execution.
- **Flexible organisation:** Assemble specialist tracks and working groups around defined objectives rather than assuming every capability requires a permanent department.
- **Shared capabilities:** Reuse suitable specialist expertise, methods, infrastructure and tools across programs where ownership and availability permit.
- **Project-based execution:** Form delivery teams around approved scope, requirements, dependencies and acceptance criteria.
- **Proportionate governance:** Apply decision controls and reporting appropriate to the scale, risk and obligations of the work.
- **Resource transparency:** Make responsibilities for resource ownership, approval, allocation and consumption explicit.
- **Outcome measurement:** Evaluate progress using defined deliverables, acceptance criteria and relevant outcome indicators.
- **Continuous improvement:** Use delivery evidence and lessons learned to improve the operating model.
- **Legal and organisational clarity:** Distinguish a logical program or project structure from a legally constituted entity or employer.

## 3. Reference Operating Model

The logical workflow is:

**Strategic Intent → Operating Model → Portfolio Priorities → Programs → Capabilities and Tracks → Working Groups and Teams → Projects → Deliverables → Outcomes**

This is a conceptual relationship model rather than a mandatory reporting hierarchy. A capability or specialist track may support several programs, and a project may draw on multiple working groups and external partners.

### Strategic Intent

Defines the mission, priorities, intended beneficiaries and outcomes that guide the organisation or ecosystem.

Strategic intent should be translated into explicit decision criteria rather than treated as a substitute for project requirements.

### Operating Model

Defines how decisions are made, how responsibilities are distributed, how capabilities are coordinated and how work progresses from approved priorities to delivery.

It establishes the relationship between governance, resources, people, processes, technology and outcomes.

### Portfolio

Groups programs and investments according to strategic objectives, resource constraints, dependencies and expected value.

Portfolio decisions may include prioritisation, resource envelopes, investment choices and continuation or termination of initiatives.

### Programs

Coordinate related projects and shared capabilities that contribute to defined outcomes.

Programs may manage dependencies, delivery sequencing, shared risks, common interfaces and consolidated outcome reporting.

### Capabilities

Represent the ability to perform a function or deliver a defined result through people, processes, knowledge, technology, IP, infrastructure or combinations of these.

Each capability should have a defined scope, responsible owner or custodian, availability status, dependencies and relevant evidence.

### Specialist Tracks

Provide continuing areas of expertise or coordination, such as engineering, research, governance, industry, commercialisation or technology platforms.

A track can maintain methods, reusable assets, technical standards and specialist knowledge across multiple programs.

### Working Groups and Teams

Bring together the people and capabilities required to perform defined work.

Working groups may be temporary or continuing, advisory or delivery-oriented. Their purpose, membership, authority, outputs and review arrangements should be documented.

### Projects

Organise approved work around a defined need, scope, deliverables, schedule, resources, risks and acceptance criteria.

Projects are the principal mechanism through which the operating model produces specific outputs and evidence.

### Outcomes

Represent the intended effects of delivered work, assessed using appropriate indicators and evidence.

Completing a project deliverable does not necessarily prove that a wider economic, public or commercial outcome has been achieved.

## 4. Reference Workflow

**Strategic Assessment → Operating Model Definition → Portfolio Prioritisation → Program Formation → Capability Mapping → Role and Team Assignment → Project Authorisation → Delivery → Outcome Measurement → Operating Model Improvement**

### Stage 1 — Strategic Assessment

Identify the need, intended beneficiaries, strategic priorities, constraints and desired outcomes.

Record the evidence supporting the need and distinguish confirmed facts from assumptions and hypotheses.

### Stage 2 — Operating Model Definition

Define the decision structure, core responsibilities, governance boundaries, shared capabilities and principal delivery processes.

Identify which functions should be centralised, shared, federated or assigned directly to projects.

### Stage 3 — Portfolio Prioritisation

Evaluate proposed programs and projects against strategic alignment, expected outcomes, resource availability, risk, dependencies and investment criteria.

Record decisions and the accountable authority.

### Stage 4 — Program Formation

Define each program's purpose, scope, expected outcomes, participating projects, shared capabilities, dependencies and governance arrangements.

### Stage 5 — Capability Mapping

Identify the capabilities needed for each program and project.

Distinguish capabilities already available from those requiring development, acquisition, partner engagement or further assessment.

### Stage 6 — Role and Team Assignment

Assign accountable owners, specialist contributors, working groups and delivery responsibilities.

Document decision rights and escalation paths. Participation or professional membership alone does not establish authority or suitability for a particular assignment.

### Stage 7 — Project Authorisation

Establish project scope, deliverables, acceptance criteria, dependencies, resources, budget authority, risk controls and required approvals.

### Stage 8 — Delivery

Execute the approved work through the relevant project lifecycle and coordinate shared dependencies across teams and programs.

### Stage 9 — Outcome Measurement

Assess deliverables and outcomes against the defined measures.

Record results, limitations, deviations, unresolved risks and lessons learned.

### Stage 10 — Operating Model Improvement

Use evidence to adjust responsibilities, workflows, capability arrangements, governance and resource allocation.

Changes should be approved by the relevant authority and communicated to affected programs and projects.

## 5. Inputs

Typical inputs include:

- Strategic priorities, mission and intended outcomes.
- Validated business, government, industry, research or internal needs.
- Portfolio objectives and investment constraints.
- Program proposals and project definitions.
- Capability catalogues and availability information.
- Existing organisational roles, processes and governance arrangements.
- Specialist skills, partner contributions and resource requirements.
- Budget, schedule, dependency and risk constraints.
- Applicable legal, employment, contractual, IP, privacy and security obligations.
- Performance indicators, acceptance criteria and evidence requirements.

The required input set should be proportionate to the scale and risk of the initiative.

## 6. Outputs

Expected outputs may include:

- Operating model description and governance boundaries.
- Portfolio and program definitions.
- Capability maps and capability ownership records.
- Specialist track definitions and working-group charters.
- Role descriptions, responsibility assignments and decision matrices.
- Project definitions, delivery arrangements and dependency records.
- Resource requirements and allocation decisions.
- Performance indicators and outcome-measurement plans.
- Risk, issue, decision and change records.
- Lessons learned and approved operating-model improvements.

The outputs required in a specific implementation should be defined by its scope and applicable governance arrangements.

## 7. Logical Interface Contracts

| Interface | Expected information |
|---|---|
| Strategy → Operating Model | Mission, priorities, constraints and intended outcomes |
| Operating Model → Portfolio | Decision boundaries, operating principles and resource governance |
| Portfolio → Program | Priorities, approved scope, constraints and outcome expectations |
| Program → Capability Owners | Required functions, dependencies, timing and acceptance expectations |
| Capability Owner → Program | Capability scope, availability, ownership, constraints and evidence |
| Program → Working Groups | Objectives, work packages, responsibilities and decision boundaries |
| Working Group → Program | Recommendations, deliverables, risks and progress evidence |
| Project → Program | Delivery status, dependencies, acceptance results and outcome measures |
| Program → Portfolio | Consolidated progress, risks, resource utilisation and outcome evidence |
| Outcome Review → Operating Model | Performance findings, improvement proposals and change requests |

These are conceptual interfaces. They may be implemented using documents, role and responsibility matrices, catalogues, workflow systems, APIs or other suitable mechanisms.

## 8. Roles and Decision Rights

The operating model should distinguish responsibility for making decisions from responsibility for carrying out work.

The following are logical roles; they do not imply that HoldCo has already appointed individuals to them.

| Role | Typical responsibility |
|---|---|
| Board or governing authority | Strategic direction, reserved decisions and oversight within its authority |
| Portfolio owner | Portfolio priorities, investment recommendations and portfolio-level monitoring |
| Program owner | Program outcomes, dependencies, coordination and program-level reporting |
| Capability owner | Capability definition, availability, maintenance and permitted-use conditions |
| Track lead | Specialist coordination, methods, standards and cross-project knowledge |
| Working-group lead | Defined work package, collaboration and agreed outputs |
| Project owner or manager | Project execution, scope, delivery coordination, risks and reporting |
| Product owner | Product priorities, requirements and product-level decisions |
| Assurance or control function | Independent review or control activities where required |

A person may hold more than one role where appropriate, provided conflicts of interest, approval boundaries and separation-of-duty requirements are addressed.

The actual authority of each role must be documented. A conceptual role does not automatically confer legal, financial, contractual or employment authority.

## 9. Responsibility and Governance Model

For significant decisions, identify:

- Who is accountable for the result.
- Who is authorised to approve the decision.
- Who performs the work.
- Who must be consulted.
- Who must be informed.
- What evidence supports the decision.
- What limits, conditions or escalation routes apply.

A responsibility matrix may be used where multiple parties contribute to the same outcome.

Governance should cover, where applicable:

- Strategy and investment decisions.
- Program and project approval.
- Budget and resource allocation.
- Recruitment, engagement and contractor arrangements.
- Procurement and partner selection.
- IP ownership, licensing and confidentiality.
- Security, privacy, safety and information access.
- Risk acceptance, issue escalation and change control.
- Deliverable acceptance and outcome reporting.

Government projects, regulated activities and cross-jurisdictional operations may require additional controls. These cannot be inferred from the operating model alone.

## 10. Capability and Team Lifecycle

A capability or specialist team may progress through the following lifecycle:

**Identified → Assessed → Assigned an Owner → Made Available → Allocated to Work → Demonstrated → Evaluated → Maintained, Reused or Retired**

The lifecycle may be adapted to the type of capability.

Record relevant information such as:

- Capability identifier, purpose and scope.
- Responsible owner or custodian.
- Required and available skills or resources.
- Dependencies, constraints and interfaces.
- Availability and allocation conditions.
- Maturity and supporting evidence.
- Access, IP and licensing conditions.
- Cost and support arrangements.
- Review date, version and known limitations.

A capability should not be described as available or validated solely because it appears in an organisation chart, catalogue or plan.

## 11. Resource and Economic Alignment

The operating model should connect organisational responsibilities with resource ownership and use.

HoldCo may define common capabilities at the portfolio or ecosystem level and allocate them to programs and projects under agreed conditions.

Relevant resource categories may include:

- Shared specialist expertise and methods.
- Software, subscriptions and engineering tools.
- IP, reusable components and product assets.
- Cloud, computing and digital infrastructure.
- Physical equipment, facilities and partner resources.
- Project-specific people, services and operational capacity.

For each shared resource, distinguish its owner, approval authority, allocation mechanism, usage record and cost responsibility.

Centralisation, virtualisation and resource pooling may improve reuse and utilisation, but their economic benefits should be measured rather than assumed.

## 12. Validation and Acceptance Criteria

Assess the reference model and its implementation using observable evidence.

| Check | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|
| Strategic alignment | Programs and projects trace to documented objectives |
| Accountability | Owners and decision authorities are identifiable |
| Structural clarity | Portfolio, program, track, working-group and project responsibilities are distinguishable |
| Capability mapping | Required and available capabilities are recorded |
| Role clarity | Responsibilities, approval boundaries and escalation routes are documented |
| Resource governance | Ownership, allocation and cost responsibilities are defined |
| Delivery traceability | Projects have scope, deliverables and acceptance criteria |
| Outcome measurement | Measures and evidence sources are defined |
| Governance coverage | Applicable controls and outstanding approvals are recorded |
| Continuous improvement | Review findings and approved changes are traceable |

A criterion listed here is not evidence that it has passed. Results should be recorded as passed, failed, partially satisfied, not assessed or not applicable, with supporting evidence and limitations.

## 15. Evidence and Maturity

**Initial status: Designed / Documented — implementation and validation status to be assessed.**

Use evidence-based maturity categories:

- **Designed / Documented:** The model and responsibilities are described.
- **Partially Implemented:** Some specified structures or workflows exist, with gaps recorded.
- **Implemented — Verification Pending:** Relevant implementation exists but verification is incomplete.
- **Demonstrated:** A defined organisational or program scenario has been executed and documented.
- **Validated:** Relevant acceptance criteria have been evaluated against sufficient evidence.

Record the assessment scope, date, evidence sources, limitations and outstanding issues.

Do not infer government adoption, procurement eligibility, funding approval, legal or regulatory compliance, commercial demand or validated economic benefits from conceptual alignment alone.
