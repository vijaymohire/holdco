# Phase 6 — QAI Advantage & Value Evaluation

## 1. Phase Identity

**Phase:** Phase 6

**Name:** QAI Advantage & Value Evaluation

**Pilot:** FAEP / QAI Digital Farm Pilot

**Primary Objective:**
Compare QAI-enabled, AI-enabled, hybrid, and classical approaches using a common problem representation, common constraints, common scenarios, and common evaluation criteria, and measure demonstrated technical, operational, and economic value.

**Phase Exit:**
Formal review and approval before progression to Phase 7 — Model Validation & Promotion.

---

## 2. Phase Purpose

Phase 6 establishes the controlled evaluation layer for determining whether QAI or QAI/Hybrid approaches provide measurable advantage over appropriate classical and AI baselines.

The phase does not assume that QAI will outperform classical or AI approaches.

The purpose is to establish evidence.

The evaluation must be capable of producing any of the following outcomes:

- QAI Advantage Demonstrated
- Potential QAI Advantage
- No Demonstrated QAI Advantage
- Classical Solution Preferred
- AI/Hybrid Solution Preferred
- Further Research Required

## 4. Governing Principle

> **Measure the advantage before claiming the advantage.**

QAI must be evaluated against a meaningful baseline.

A QAI result is not automatically an advantage simply because it uses a quantum algorithm, quantum-compatible representation, quantum simulator, or quantum hardware.

Advantage must be demonstrated through defined evidence.

## 26. Fair Comparison Principle

The evaluation must avoid unfair comparisons.

Examples of unfair comparison include:

- different problem sizes
- different constraints
- different input quality
- different stopping criteria
- different hardware assumptions without recording them
- deliberately weak classical baselines
- selective scenario reporting
- reporting only successful QAI results
- ignoring failed or infeasible QAI runs

Negative results are valid evidence.

## 29. Advantage Gate

The Advantage Gate is the principal Phase 6 decision mechanism.

Possible outcomes are:

### GREEN — QAI Advantage Demonstrated

Evidence demonstrates a meaningful advantage under defined conditions.

### BLUE — Potential QAI Advantage

Promising result exists, but evidence is insufficient for a strong advantage claim.

### AMBER — No Demonstrated Advantage

QAI approach is valid but no measurable advantage has been demonstrated.

### CLASSICAL PREFERRED

The classical approach provides the better overall solution for the evaluated use case.

### FURTHER RESEARCH

The problem, data, algorithm, hardware, or maturity is insufficient for a meaningful conclusion.

---

## 31. No-Advantage Outcome

A result showing no QAI advantage is an acceptable Phase 6 outcome.

It can establish:

- the classical baseline remains preferable
- QAI requires further research
- the problem is not suitable for QAI at current maturity
- the QAI formulation needs improvement
- hardware or runtime limitations dominate
- economic value does not justify QAI adoption

Such results should be retained as evidence.

## 32. Classical Fallback

The classical solution remains the fallback path.

    QAI / Hybrid
         ↓

         ↓

         │

              ↓

              ↓
         Continue operation

This ensures that QAI experimentation does not compromise system continuity.

## 33. AI / Hybrid Fallback

Where AI or hybrid methods provide better results than either purely classical or QAI approaches, the architecture should permit the AI/hybrid solution to become the preferred path.

The objective is not to force a particular technology.

The objective is to select the most appropriate validated solution.
